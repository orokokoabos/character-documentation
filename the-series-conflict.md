# The Series Conflict

## Overview

The central conflict of the series revolves around an incurable disease called **Tarlylox**.

Tarlylox can infect both humans and demi-humans. However, there is an important difference between the two groups:

- Infected demi-humans can transmit Tarlylox to other demi-humans and humans.
- Infected humans cannot transmit Tarlylox to anyone else.

There is currently no cure for the disease. Nearly everyone who contracts it will die within a few years.

This creates an extremely complicated political, social, medical, and moral conflict. Demi-humans pose a genuine public-health risk because they can transmit an incurable and fatal disease. However, most demi-humans are also victims of the disease. They did not choose to become infected, cannot control the fact that they can transmit it, and cannot simply decide to stop carrying it.

Humans who become infected are victims as well. They face a slow death from an incurable disease, even though they cannot transmit it to anyone else.

The central question of the series is therefore:

**How should the world respond to demi-humans and the threat of Tarlylox?**

There is no universally accepted answer. Different people, nations, organizations, and communities respond according to their own beliefs, fears, interests, prejudices, and circumstances.

# Tarlylox

## Transmission

Tarlylox can infect both humans and demi-humans.

Demi-humans can transmit the disease to other people, including both humans and other demi-humans. For reasons that have not yet been discovered, humans who contract Tarlylox cannot transmit it further.

This makes humans dead-end hosts for the disease, while demi-humans remain capable of spreading it.

The reason for this biological difference is currently unknown within the story.

## Progression and Symptoms

Tarlylox progresses slowly, usually killing an infected person within a few years.

During most of the disease’s progression, the infected person’s physical condition gradually deteriorates. Their symptoms include:

- Increasing fatigue
- Progressive loss of physical strength
- Reduced stamina
- Darkening of the lungs
- Shriveling and deterioration of the lungs

These symptoms continue to worsen throughout approximately 99% of the disease’s progression.

Near the final stage, the infected person’s cells begin to break down. This cellular deterioration becomes increasingly severe over several weeks and ultimately results in death.

Tarlylox is effectively 100% fatal.

## Treatment

There is currently no cure for Tarlylox.

Treatments have been developed that can slow the disease’s progression for a reasonable amount of time, potentially extending an infected person’s life by several months. Medical treatment is gradually improving, but it cannot eliminate the disease or prevent the infected person’s eventual death.

At present, everyone who contracts Tarlylox is expected to die from it within a few years.

# Humans and Demi-Humans

## Humans

Humans in the series are ordinary humans as we understand them in real life (IRL humans, not in the story).

They can become infected with Tarlylox and die from it, but they cannot transmit the disease to other people.

## Demi-Humans

Demi-humans are different types of humanlike people. They generally resemble humans but possess biological, supernatural, or physical characteristics that distinguish them from ordinary humans.

Examples of demi-humans may include:

- Cat-boys and cat-girls
- Humans with bunny ears
- Humans with bear ears
- Humans with dragon tails or wings
- Elves
- Vampires
- Demons

Demons are the only ones that naturally are more resistant to the disease, giving a few extra years, however they all die eventually still.

“Demi-human” does not simply mean that a species has a humanoid body. Not every humanoid species is classified as demi-human. For example, orcs and goblins are not considered demi-humans.

Demi-humans may be understood as human-adjacent peoples or, in some cases, as different human subspecies. However, the demi-human category is broader than a simple biological definition because it can include groups such as elves, vampires, and demons.

The exact classification depends on the rules and history of the setting.

# The Central Moral and Political Problem

Demi-humans can transmit a fatal disease for which no cure exists. This means their presence can create a genuine risk to public health.

However, demi-humans are usually victims rather than willing spreaders of the disease. They cannot control the fact that they have Tarlylox, and they cannot magically choose to stop transmitting it. If they are infected, then they are infected.

Humans who contract the disease are also innocent victims. They may become infected through exposure to a demi-human and then face a slow, unavoidable death.

This creates a conflict in which protecting one group can place another group at risk, while attempting to protect humans can result in the persecution, displacement, imprisonment, or death of innocent demi-humans.

The world must decide how to respond, but no response is universally accepted.

---

# Responses to Demi-Humans

Different nations, kingdoms, countries, organizations, and communities respond to demi-humans in different ways.

Some governments exile demi-humans from their territory. Demi-humans may be removed from their homes and forced to leave the country, even if they have lived there their entire lives.

Some governments imprison or isolate them to prevent them from coming into contact with the wider population.

Some governments kill demi-humans because they believe eliminating them is the only way to permanently stop the spread of Tarlylox.

Other governments allow demi-humans to remain but place them in designated areas separated from other civilians. These governments may attempt to accommodate demi-humans while also reducing the possibility of transmission.

Some nations exclude demi-humans because they sincerely believe that allowing them to enter would create an unacceptable public-health risk. Their leaders may feel sympathy for demi-humans while still believing that exclusion is necessary to protect their citizens.

Other nations or political groups may already be prejudiced against demi-humans and use Tarlylox as a convenient justification for harming them. They may publicly claim that demi-humans are a danger to public health while concealing racist, malicious, or politically motivated intentions.

The motivations behind anti-demi-human policies therefore vary considerably.

Some people are frightened.

Some genuinely believe that exclusion or separation is necessary.

Some feel sorry for demi-humans but believe they cannot safely accommodate them.

Some are openly prejudiced.

Others disguise their prejudice as concern for public safety.

Even when two nations adopt similar policies, they may do so for entirely different reasons.

---

# Pro-Demi-Human Nations and Movements

Some nations and organizations are strongly pro-demi-human.

They attempt to accommodate demi-humans, defend their rights, and allow them to remain part of society. They may provide protected areas, medical support, housing, or other forms of assistance.

Some governments make discriminatory conduct or hate speech against demi-humans illegal.

These policies do not always receive universal public support. Citizens may protest against their government’s willingness to accommodate demi-humans because they fear becoming infected with Tarlylox.

Other citizens may strongly support demi-human rights and believe that persecuting innocent people for carrying a disease they cannot control is morally unacceptable.

Public opinion differs across the world and within individual countries.

Attempts to accommodate demi-humans may sometimes result in additional infections and deaths. Conversely, removing demi-humans from society may leave them homeless, without food or medical care, and separated from the only communities they have ever known.

A government may save some lives through exclusion while condemning innocent demi-humans to die elsewhere. Another government may protect demi-humans but expose part of its human population to a greater risk of infection.

These consequences make the conflict difficult to reduce to a simple division between good and evil.

---

# The Global Conflict

The existence of Tarlylox creates widespread fear, political instability, discrimination, activism, violence, and war.

No one can agree on what should be done.

Different nations support different policies. Different organizations advocate for competing solutions. Even people who recognize the same facts about the disease may reach completely different moral and political conclusions.

Some believe demi-humans must be protected.

Some believe they should be separated from humans.

Some believe they must be expelled.

Some believe they must be imprisoned.

Some believe exterminating them is the only way to guarantee humanity’s survival.

Others believe extermination is genocide and that the world must find a way to protect both populations, regardless of the risks involved.

The intentions of the people involved are equally varied. A person may support demi-human exclusion because of sincere fear, calculated public-health reasoning, political ambition, personal prejudice, or some combination of these motives.

Likewise, a person may support demi-human integration because of compassion, civil-rights principles, political interests, personal relationships, or confidence that the risks can be managed.

This unresolved disagreement forms the core conflict of the series.

# Structure of the Series

The series contains at least fifty arcs and follows dozens of protagonists and antagonists.

It does not have one permanent protagonist whose perspective controls the entire story. The central viewpoint can change every few arcs, allowing the story to explore the Tarlylox conflict from many different places and social positions.

The protagonists may include:

- Researchers
- Farmers
- Fighters and soldiers
- Poor people
- Wealthy people
- Political leaders
- Activists
- Ordinary civilians
- Pro-demi-human figures
- Anti-demi-human figures

There are protagonists on multiple sides of the conflict. A protagonist is not automatically morally correct simply because the story follows their perspective.

Some protagonists die. Others survive and appear again later. Some disappear from the story entirely after completing their role.

The characters do not all know one another. Some live in different countries or on different continents. Some never meet and may not even know that the other person exists.

Nevertheless, their actions can still contribute to the larger story.

A protagonist may die during one arc, but their research, ideas, political actions, discoveries, failures, or sacrifices may create an opportunity for another protagonist in a later arc.

Sometimes the consequences appear immediately in the next arc. In other cases, several unrelated arcs may pass before another character builds upon that person’s work.

A character introduced early in the series may unexpectedly reappear or be mentioned much later. This creates the sense that the world and its history continue to exist beyond the boundaries of any individual arc.

Not every arc is directly connected to the arc before or after it. Some arcs may initially appear completely unrelated. However, each arc contributes something to the world, the history of the conflict, or the circumstances inherited by later characters.

The connections are not always based on characters personally knowing one another. Instead, the arcs are connected through their consequences.

A researcher’s discovery may influence a government years later.

A farmer’s death may contribute to political unrest.

A military victory in one country may change the balance of power somewhere else.

A protagonist may build upon the work of someone they never met.

An apparently minor event may become important many arcs later.

The series therefore functions as a large interconnected narrative made up of many individual stories. Each protagonist experiences only one part of the world, but their choices collectively shape the broader conflict surrounding Tarlylox.

The story explores this conflict from many perspectives without limiting it to a single nation, ideology, social class, or permanent group of heroes.
