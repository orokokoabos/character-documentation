# Edward's Arc

## Scope and Narrative Position

Edward's arc takes place directly after the [TTC arc](./TTC.md). It follows [Captain Edward Baker](../other-characters/captain-edward.md) and his crew. Apart from their missions, the principal setting is one of the [Grand Navy's](../world-powers/the-grand-navy.md) bases. The Navy has multiple bases and headquarters; this is one particular posting.

**This document records only selected major events and the context needed for this repository. It is not a complete account of the arc.** Many missions, relationships, conflicts, and other important story events are omitted. Their omission does not mean that they are unimportant to the story, it's just unimportant as context to help explain Francesca. They do not need to be fully documented in a repository focused on Francesca Badu.

The arc is included because it establishes Edward, the crew's shared history, and the founding of [Foxden Co.](../world-powers/organizations-and-entities/foxden.md). Edward and Foxden later participate in the conflict with Leon, whose movement is directly relevant to Francesca's role in the final war. Francesca also has one significant, separate incident within this arc.

## Edward and His New Crew

Edward is already a veteran captain who has held the rank for a long time when he receives a new team. The team has recently lost its previous captain.

Edward comforts its members and begins working with them, but their adjustment is difficult. They are accustomed to their former captain's orders and methods of coordination. Edward must learn to lead this particular group and help them work effectively together under a different captain.

Much of the first portion of the arc concerns this process. Through numerous missions and interactions, they gradually develop their coordination, trust, and close personal bonds. The crew becomes deeply attached to Edward and to one another.

This is a broad summary of a substantial part of the arc, rather than an inventory of its individual missions and events. Those accumulated experiences establish the relationships that make the later hostage crisis personally significant.

## Francesca's Background Presence at the Base

This arc occurs during [teenage Francesca's period](../francesca-badu/francesca-timeline.md). She is assigned to the same base as Edward.

**Francesca is a separate Navy Captain. She is not a member of Edward's crew.** At this time she has no crew of her own. The Navy does not consider her particularly good at coordinating other people and prefers not to assign her a crew; she generally undertakes her missions alone.

For almost the entire arc, Francesca has less involvement than a side character. She occasionally appears in the background when Edward and his crew are at the base, or becomes the subject of a passing conversation. She does not accompany their missions or drive the crew's central conflict.

Francesca is exceptionally well known within this particular base because of her extraordinary beauty. She is effectively its most popular young woman, attracting widespread admiration and romantic interest. Edward and his crew sometimes notice her and discuss how attractive she is.

These appearances also present the favorable impression people have of her: beautiful, kind, humble, and apparently admirable. Her popularity at this base should not be treated as a claim that she personally knows everyone throughout the entire Grand Navy.

Apart from the separate mission described next, she is essentially background presence. That one incident does not make her a main character or a regular side character in Edward's arc.

## Francesca's Trafficking Mission and the Imprisoned Children

### The Cafeteria Transition

At one point, Edward and his crew notice that Francesca is absent from the cafeteria at a time when she usually eats there.

While waiting for food, they ask other people in the cafeteria about her absence and learn that she is away on a mission. The narrative then switches to Francesca's operation against an underground human-trafficking organization.

Following the favorable impressions established through her background appearances and other people's conversations, this sequence provides the first direct look at how she actually thinks.

### Defeating the Traffickers and Leaving the Children

Francesca defeats the criminals involved in the organization. As described in her psychological documentation, the operation is both her job and an opportunity to reinforce her desired reputation as an extraordinarily capable, heroic Navy Captain.

The organization has imprisoned enslaved children in cells. After defeating the traffickers, Francesca walks past the children without freeing them.

Their captivity and suffering do not intrinsically matter to her. Her attention has been on defeating the criminals and demonstrating her abilities; rescuing the children for additional recognition does not occur to her at that moment.

Afterward, she recognizes a possible reputational problem: if someone else eventually rescues them, the children could report that she saw them and deliberately left them imprisoned.

She returns and kills them to eliminate that possibility.

She could recognize alternatives such as returning to rescue them and explaining her earlier departure, but those alternatives would leave witnesses who could still describe what they saw. Under this incident's established circumstances, she chooses to eliminate that uncertainty. The children's lives provide no intrinsic emotional reason for her to reject doing so.

The full explanation of her selective attention, concern for reputation, emotional indifference, and the investigative circumstances is in [The Ant Analogy — The Imprisoned Children](../francesca-badu/psychology/the-ant-analogy.md#example-the-imprisoned-children). This is an actual story event, distinct from the hypothetical scenarios in that document.

She kills each child with a single kick. Her movements look casual and require little apparent effort; her strength makes each blow immediately fatal. The sequence does not depict prolonged torture or an elaborate, exaggerated attack.

This incident takes place separately from Edward's crew's activities. It is a notable event within his arc, but it is not their mission or part of their later hostage crisis.

## The Pirates and the Captured Crew Members

During a later mission, Edward and his crew fight a dangerous group of pirates. The Navy crew wins the fighting on land, but the pirates manage to take approximately one or two crew members hostage as they retreat.

The pirates bring the captives aboard their ship. They use them to prevent Edward's crew from destroying the departing vessel: sinking it would also endanger the captured Navy members. They also threaten to kill the hostages if they are followed.

Edward cannot safely resolve the situation through an immediate attack. He and the remaining crew return to the base to develop a rescue plan and obtain authorization to mobilize their ship.

## Conflict With the Navy's Higher-Ups

### Refusal to Authorize a Rescue

Edward requests permission to mobilize the ship, pursue the pirates, and recover the captured crew members.

The Navy's higher-ups refuse. They consider the assigned mission completed and will not authorize further mobilization for the sake of the small number of captives. They also raise the risks of escalating the conflict with the pirates and creating further problems.

Peaceful negotiations fail to secure the captives' release.

To Edward and his crew, leaving them behind is unacceptable. These are people with whom they have built close bonds over the course of the arc. They regard one another as brothers, rather than as replaceable personnel whose loss can simply be accepted.

### Appeals, Protests, and Public Response

The dispute continues over several chapters as Edward and his crew seek a way to rescue their teammates.

They appeal to other personnel at the base, ask for help, and protest the refusal to act. Some Navy members support them and become willing to assist.

The higher-ups intervene, regarding the protests and recruitment of support as a distraction from other personnel's duties. They shut the effort down.

As word of the dispute spreads, the Navy holds a press conference to defend its position and explain its refusal to authorize the pursuit.

These institutional responses deepen Edward's conflict with the organization. The Navy both refuses to mount the rescue and prevents the crew from organizing an authorized response themselves.

## Desertion and Rescue

Edward challenges the contradiction he sees between the Navy's stated purpose and its treatment of the captives.

He and his supporters understand the Navy as an institution meant to enforce the law, preserve peace, and protect people. The captured personnel have served those aims. They cannot accept an organization abandoning those people after negotiations have failed, while also preventing their comrades from acting.

The risk of escalation does not make leaving their companions in captivity acceptable to them.

Edward gives a speech and gathers his own crew and other willing Navy personnel. Approximately two dozen members desert together, taking Navy ships without authorization to undertake the rescue. Some of the deserters are captains themselves.

The Navy labels them criminals.

The deserters nevertheless pursue the pirates, storm their base and prison, defeat them, and rescue the captured crew members.

Their decision follows the relationships developed throughout the arc and the prolonged failure to obtain permission. They choose their companions and their understanding of the Navy's purpose over obedience to its leadership.

## Refuge in Tarmi and the Founding of Foxden Co.

After the rescue, the deserters flee to the Kingdom of Tarmi to avoid capture by the Grand Navy.

The Navy does not automatically possess jurisdiction over every nation. It can operate in Tarmi through cooperation with the kingdom, but it cannot simply treat Tarmi as territory under its own unrestricted authority.

After hearing the deserters' explanation, Tarmi refuses to cooperate in their capture and permits them to remain in the kingdom.

Edward and the other former Navy members subsequently establish **Foxden Co.**, a private military and security company, and begin operating their business in Tarmi.

The founding group consists of Navy deserters, including several highly capable fighters and some captains. They are very strong, although that does not place them among the setting's absolute strongest combatants. Foxden's later recruitment and expansion occur beyond this arc.

**Edward's arc ends with the formation of Foxden Co.**

## Later Relevance to This Repository

Edward and Foxden recur elsewhere in the story. Their later involvement in [Leon's biological-weapon blueprint pursuit](./leon-blueprint-pursuit-and-shipwreck.md) is especially significant: they help prevent his regime from obtaining a weapon intended for mass extermination, and Leon kills Edward and many of the original deserters during that conflict.

That later role connects the history established here to Leon's movement and the final war involving Francesca. It explains why this arc belongs in this repository despite Francesca's extremely limited presence.

Many other arcs and events remain undocumented because they do not provide the context this Francesca-focused repository needs. This outline likewise leaves out a large amount of Edward's own arc; it does not replace the full story.
