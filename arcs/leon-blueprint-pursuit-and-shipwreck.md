# Leon's Biological-Weapon Blueprint Pursuit and Shipwreck

## Scope and Narrative Position

This document summarizes a Leon-focused arc during the worldwide war between the Anti-Demi-Human Coalition (Leon's Regime) and the opposing pro-demi-human forces. It is **an outline of the central events, not a complete scene-by-scene account**. Numerous other battles, interactions, and events occur during the arc but have not been documented here.

Unlike much of the wider war narrative, which shifts among numerous fronts across the world, this arc concentrates on the region containing Leon's headquarters, the development of a proposed biological weapon, the operations of a recurring private military company, and Leon's personal collapse and subsequent escalation.

The arc matters to the characterization of Francesca Badu because Leon's devastation following her answer of **"No"** helps precipitate his withdrawal from leadership, and his eventual self-serving reinterpretation of that answer helps restore his motivation. Francesca does not need to participate directly in these events for her symbolic importance to Leon to shape their consequences.

## Background: Leon After Francesca's Refusal

Leon has already asked Francesca whether she considers the extermination of demi-humans a moral obligation for the benefit of humanity. After briefly considering the question, Francesca answered simply, **"No."**

Leon, who idolizes Francesca as humanity's savior and assumes that she shares his convictions, is devastated by the answer. For weeks afterward he remains depressed and withdrawn, largely staying in his office and neglecting matters he would ordinarily oversee. His withdrawal takes place during an active worldwide war, but this document concerns the consequences on a particular local front rather than every front of the conflict.

Historically, Leon is generally composed even when receiving bad military news. He may feel anger internally, but he usually maintains a controlled, sometimes cold demeanor. His prolonged inactivity here is unusual for him and leaves him insufficiently attentive to a rapidly deteriorating local situation.

## The Proposed Biological Weapon

The Anti-Demi-Human Coalition has spent a substantial period developing and testing a biological weapon intended for mass extermination of demi-humans.

Testing has included both demi-human and human subjects, because the researchers have attempted to make the weapon lethal to demi-humans while avoiding harm to humans. **They have not succeeded.** The experimental weapon kills humans as well as demi-humans.

The weapon is meant to produce mass casualties. If deployed over an inhabited city, it could slowly and painfully kill the population: victims may struggle to breathe, their flesh darkens, and their bodies progressively deteriorate before death. These are the story's described effects, not a technical account of its mechanism.

The coalition decides to proceed despite the failure of species selectivity. Its leaders intend to avoid deploying it in areas populated by humans as much as possible, but accept that humans may also die. In their view, those casualties are a cost of achieving the larger extermination campaign.

A highly important blueprint is approaching the point at which it can be presented to Leon for approval and used to complete or implement the intended weapon. The couriers carrying it must reach Leon's headquarters. This blueprint becomes central to the arc because it is the **only physical copy established in this account**.

Leon believes that possession and eventual use of this weapon could decisively change the war and help accomplish the extermination he considers necessary for humanity's survival.

## Geography and the Private Military Company's Advance

Leon's headquarters lies in a central city, within a region containing surrounding cities, smaller nations, settlements, and villages. Some surrounding territories support the regime; others were conquered and placed under its control rather than voluntarily joining its cause.

An established, well-known private military company aligned with the pro-demi-human side operates on this front [Foxden Co.](../../world-powers/organizations-and-entities/foxden.md). The company has appeared elsewhere in the wider story, for example their military support of The Grand Navy during the Glyph vs Grand Navy arc.

The company's fighters are exceptionally powerful. A relatively small contingent can overwhelm conventional forces that vastly outnumber them; the earlier approximate description was around twenty elite individuals in the relevant fighting, with individuals potentially capable of defeating formations of hundreds of ordinary soldiers. These figures are approximate descriptions of the story's scale, not a complete roster or fixed deployment count for every operation.

During the weeks Leon spends in his office, the company and allied forces systematically dismantle the regime's control in the area. Some defenders surrender, some are arrested or taken prisoner, and some are killed while resisting.

Territories previously conquered by the regime are liberated. Some inhabitants or local forces then join the anti-regime effort, some higher ups also betray Leon to keep themselves alive and avoid going to prison. Small surrounding nations and settlements fall out of Leon's control, followed by growing pressure on the ring of cities closer to his headquarters.

Dozens of other war fronts remain active, and Leon's regime continues to possess territory elsewhere, but Leon's local situation steadily worsens until his area is effectively surrounded.

## The Blueprint Is Intercepted

The party carrying the weapon blueprint toward Leon's headquarters is intercepted by the private military company and opposing forces.

Before the physical blueprint is burned and destroyed, **a female mage among the company's personnel uses information-storage or visual-imaging magic to preserve its contents in her mind**. This is a magical preservation of the blueprint's information; no detailed mechanism for the ability has been defined here.

The mage becomes the only **known surviving holder of the blueprint's information** in this account. Her party attempts to escape the region and return to territory outside the regime's tightening local reach.

Leon learns that the couriers have been intercepted, the original blueprint has been destroyed, and the information is being carried away by an enemy mage. He also finally confronts the extent of the local military collapse that occurred while he was withdrawn.

The loss has two consequences from Leon's perspective. First, he is about to lose access to what he considers a potentially decisive weapon. Second, he fears the enemy may be able to reconstruct the design and use the information against his coalition. That feared use is a strategic possibility.

For Leon, preventing the mage's escape becomes urgent. He interprets the company's actions as removing one of humanity's best chances to survive the disease, even though the company and its allies are fighting to prevent an extermination campaign.

## Leon Abandons the Headquarters and Gives Chase

Leon mobilizes the forces still available in and around his headquarters. He does not recall his worldwide armies from unrelated fronts; the pursuit uses personnel he can obtain locally, including reinforcements that join along the way.

He gives up on holding his particular headquarters. It can be replaced with a headquarters elsewhere in the regime's remaining territory, whereas Leon believes the departing blueprint information may become unrecoverable if the mage escapes.

Leon and his forces pursue the company's group through the remaining contested area. Fighting continues during the chase. Reinforcements arrive, clashes recur, and Leon's available forces are gradually exhausted. Ultimately, **every member of his pursuing local force is killed except Leon**.

The company reaches a dock on the edge of the ring of cities. From there, a ship can cross a lake to a small nation on the far shore, already beyond Leon's accessible local territory. That nation contains only a few cities. If the group successfully crosses, Leon has no convenient boat or means of immediately following them from his side of the lake.

## The Dock: Leon Crashing Out

By this point, Leon has been seriously injured in combat. One of the company's mages has created a **black crystalline magical spike** that impales him through the abdomen and holds him in place. He cannot simply step free.

Leon's people in the immediate pursuit have all fallen. He watches the remaining company personnel move to board the ship, carrying the mage whose mind preserves the blueprint.

He believes he is watching the future of humanity disappear in front of him. He does not regard this as a mere humiliation or a routine strategic defeat. Within his worldview, the enemy is actively depriving humanity of the means to prevent its own extinction. He is furious at what he perceives as their willingness to let humanity die.

He screams/yells in anger while crying, his speech strained by sobs and increasingly hoarse, as though his voice is giving out. He threatens to pursue and "follow you across the ends of the world" to kill them. Kill the escaping fighters even if he has to chase them across the world. He's in rage, terror, disgust, and desperation simultaneously.

Basically what he's thinking is this: If the demi humans don't die, the disease will end all of humanity. The blueprint and weapon is the current best chance at exterminating the demi humans, none of them individually deserve it, and I care for their lives, but the lives of all of humanity matters more. You running away with the possible key to killing all the demi humans, which will prevent humanity from extinction, means you're preventing the survival of humanity, without this weapon, the demi humans may live, and humanity will end. I know you probably do not see it the way I see it, I don't think you WANT humanity to end, but if you do this, humanity will end, and I cannot let that happen. Why can't you see it my way, and see that this is the best chance humanity has at survival, I want to save humanity why can't you understand?

He isn't thinking logically like we just said, but this is an explanation from the authors of what he's thinking. His actual thoughts are much more messy than this but this is the core idea and rationalization.

In terms of presentation, there's no music, the fighters are about to board the ship at the dock, and Leon's raw, breaking cries carry through the surrounding quiet.

In his desperation, Leon forces himself forward by tearing his own body off the impaling spike rather than carefully removing it because he has no time. The movement destroys a substantial portion of his abdomen—roughly a third of the abdominal area and leaves him catastrophically wounded.

Despite the injury, Leon attacks. He kills several of the company's remaining personnel, but the others eventually overpower him and throw him into the lake.

## Crossing the Lake Beneath the Ship

The surviving company personnel depart by ship, unaware that Leon has not abandoned the pursuit.

Leon clings to the **underside of the ship** and is dragged across the lake beneath the water.

When the ship reaches the far shore, Leon emerges without the remaining personnel realizing he has traveled underneath them. He attacks again and kills the remaining escorts before they realise he's here.

He deliberately does not kill the mage who memorized the blueprint. She is the person who holds the surviving design information, and Leon intends to extract that information from her by torture if necessary so the regime can still build the weapon.

Leon takes her captive, returns to the ship, and turns it away from the landing. He does not head back to his original headquarters, which he has abandoned and may have lost. He sails without a firm destination, desperately hoping to reach another territory held by his regime and receive medical care.

## The Shipwreck and Leon's Recovery

Leon eventually loses consciousness because of catastrophic blood loss. With him unconscious, the ship crashes.

The mage dies in the crash.

**The crash takes place in territory controlled by Leon's regime.** Leon is found by his own side, receives medical treatment, and survives. He regains consciousness **after** he has already been given care; he does not wake up stranded and untreated at the wreck.

Upon learning that the captured mage died, Leon realizes that the information he fought so desperately to recover has been lost. The only known surviving mental copy of the blueprint is gone with her. His desperate pursuit has therefore failed to recover the weapon design, despite the lives lost and his own near-fatal injuries.

The loss marks the end of the immediate blueprint pursuit, but **not the end of Leon's extermination campaign or the worldwide war**.

## Aftermath: Reinterpreting Francesca's Answer

Remember Francesca's answer "No" to his question: [Francesca's Opinion on Morals](../francesca-badu/psychology/opinions-on-morals.md)

Following his awakening and learning of the mage's death, Leon reconsiders Francesca's earlier answer.

Rather than accepting her simple "No" as sincere disagreement with his claimed moral obligation to exterminate demi-humans, he convinces himself that she **must have had some deeper reason or meaning** beyond what he presently understands. This interpretation is his own rationalization, not a newly revealed message from Francesca.

The rationalization restores his sense of purpose and regains the motivation and drive after being depressed in his office for weeks. He recommits himself to purging demi-humans and decides that the constraints he had previously observed must no longer prevent him from pursuing what he considers humanity's survival.

## Psychological and Military Transformation After the Shipwreck

This is a significant shift in Leon's behavior, decision-making, and devotion to Francesca following the shipwreck and his waking up after.

## Leon's Usual Conduct Before the Incident

Throughout most earlier arcs, Leon is generally **calm, controlled, and sometimes cold**, even when confronted with military losses or serious setbacks. He may be angry internally, but he usually manages his outward reaction and deals with the situation in a composed manner.

He is also capable of ordinary enjoyment and social interaction. His leadership role and harsh ideology do not mean he is serious every moment of every day. At times he makes cheeky or smug remarks, jokes during meals, or participates in casual conversation. This occasional humor is part of his established personality before the shipwreck.

His devotion to Francesca is already extraordinarily intense: he reveres her as humanity's savior, is deeply infatuated with her, and helped establish her near-divine status among the regime's followers. None of this begins with the shipwreck.

## His Reinterpretation of Francesca's "No"

Leon resolves the emotional conflict caused by Francesca's answer through **self-deception**.

He decides that her "No" must conceal some deeper meaning or intention that he cannot presently understand. Since he regards Francesca as vastly superior to himself, he uses the possibility of an inaccessible deeper explanation to preserve both his worship of her and his belief that extermination is necessary. Even though Francesca wasn't saying anything deep, she just said "No" and that's all she thought. Leon is just making this up in his head basically.

This rationalization restores his motivation. He recommits to his original objective and becomes more uncompromising in pursuing it.

## Abandonment of Previous Military Restraints

Leon and the Anti-Demi-Human Coalition have **already committed atrocities and war crimes** before this point, the World Government and Pro Demi Humans have as well. The shift is not from humane warfare to inhumane warfare, nor from a reluctance to kill to a sudden desire for violence.

Earlier in the war, however, Leon had refrained from authorizing certain particularly extreme methods because of their enormous costs and consequences, including the sacrifice of his own personnel. The World Government and its allies have also committed war crimes during the desperate worldwide conflict, but that does not mean either side has used every conceivable atrocity.

After his recovery, Leon authorizes and encourages the immediate use of **any means he believes necessary** to advance extermination, including methods he previously withheld.

### Example: Mass-Sacrifice Mage Rituals

One existing form of large-scale attack in the setting involves **hundreds of mages sacrificing their lives in a ritual** to produce an area-of-effect strike capable of destroying a metropolis and killing enormous numbers of people.

These rituals are not an invention Leon suddenly discovers after waking up. They are among the extreme options already available in the setting. Their devastating human cost and status as war crimes are reasons they have generally been avoided in the war up to this point.

Leon now authorizes such attacks. His regime intends to use **captured mages first** as the required sacrifices. If there are not enough prisoners, the regime is also prepared to sacrifice its **own mages and personnel**.

There are other possible methods for rapidly destroying entire cities, but they likewise have terrible drawbacks, costs, or legal and moral consequences. Leon's new stance is that these considerations will no longer prevent the use of a method he thinks will further the campaign.

### Ranks and Large-Scale Destructive Power

In the setting, some individuals at **S, SS, SSS, or G rank** may be capable of destruction on a similar metropolitan scale through their own abilities, **provided their particular abilities include sufficiently powerful large-area attacks**. Rank alone does not guarantee a city-destroying area-of-effect technique.

Such combatants are extremely rare. Approximate, **not finalized**, figures discussed for the world are around one hundred S-rank individuals, perhaps fifty individuals across SS and SSS, and roughly only a few G-rank individuals (at this point in time, because most of them end up dying to Francesca in the future during the final battles 💀)

**Leon himself is S-rank**, but his ability set does **not** include the kind of enormous area-of-effect attack that would let him personally accomplish the same citywide destruction. His importance here is his authorization and coordination of catastrophic methods, not an undocumented ability to cast such an attack himself.

## Anger and Emotional Regulation After the Shipwreck

Before this turning point, Leon generally stays calm when his regime loses battles or faces setbacks. 

Afterward, bad news frequently provokes open anger.

He may shout at his officers, scream profanities, slam tables, and berate those around him after defeats or other major negative developments. These are the kinds of situations to which the earlier Leon would generally have responded with controlled coldness.

He is not continuously shouting or enraged, outside such triggers, his behavior can be quiet and withdrawn. What has changed is his ability or willingness to contain anger when events go badly.

His deterioration should be evident through contrast with the earlier Leon.

## Loss of Humor and Ordinary Social Warmth

The earlier Leon is occasionally cheeky, smug, amused, or willing to joke with other people—for example, during dinner. This is ordinary personality, not evidence that he takes the war lightly.

After the shipwreck, this side of him is effectively gone. He speaks much less and no longer participates in the lighthearted interactions he once enjoyed. His daily attention becomes almost exclusively directed toward killing demi-humans and winning the extermination campaign.

## Francesca's Photograph and Private Prayer

Leon was already responsible for encouraging the regime's reverence for Francesca as a savior or godlike figure. Following his recovery, his personal devotion becomes still more consuming.

He keeps a **printed photograph of Francesca** and carries it with him wherever he goes. When he has nothing immediate to do, he takes out or looks at the photograph and stares at it with a largely expressionless face.

While looking at it, he silently hopes or prays for a miracle.
