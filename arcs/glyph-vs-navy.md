# Glyph vs the Grand Navy

## Scope and Narrative Position

This is a major war arc involving the [Grand Navy](../world-powers/the-grand-navy.md), the [Albagliff Empire (Glyph)](../world-powers/glyph-empire.md), the [Assassin Syndicate](../world-powers/the-assassin-syndicate.md), the [World Government](../world-powers/organizations-and-entities/world-government.md), **and many other smaller forces that will not be named to keep it shorter, we named the biggest ones**. It follows [Francesca vs the Assassin Syndicate](./francesca-vs-assassin-syndicate.md).

**This document deliberately omits a very large amount of the arc: events, battles, character stories, and entire sub-arcs. The material selected here is selected for its relevance to Francesca, not because it represents all the most important events of the war.** Many omitted events are central to the war and its protagonists while contributing little to an explanation of Francesca.

Some of those wider developments are summarized only far enough to make her circumstances and the later convergence understandable. A short summary here may stand for dozens of sub-arcs or hundreds of chapters. 

The war follows several protagonists. One is [Gektor](../other-characters/gektor.md), leader of Glyph's national hero team. Two other principal wartime viewpoints are not individually documented here because their stories do not directly provide the Francesca-related context this repository needs. One of the war's protagonists dies; this outline does not assign that statement to an otherwise unspecified individual.

Francesca is absent from much of the wider narrative. Her actions help establish the conditions for the conflict, but she is not its central participant or the person directing either side.

**This war is separate from the later worldwide war involving Leon's regime. The G-Rank Free For All is a sub-arc within this war. It does not, by itself, resolve the Navy–Glyph conflict.**

## The Crisis Following the Assassin Syndicate Confrontation

Following the preceding arc, the Grand Navy has lost approximately one-quarter of its overall institutional power. The damage includes personnel losses and deaths, equipment losses, and diminished operational capability. This is an approximate description of institutional losses, not an exact casualty percentage applying equally to every department.

Francesca has been dismissed from the Navy. The circumstances of her command of a fleet, the confrontation with the Syndicate, and her dismissal belong in the prerequisite arc.

The Assassin Syndicate is also recovering from its confrontation with Francesca and the Navy fleet. It is furious about what the Navy has done and has additional reason to act against it.

Meanwhile, the Navy's long-standing tension with Glyph persists. Their historical relationship is explained in [the Navy's history](../world-powers/the-grand-navy.md). Powerful criminal organizations, whose activities have been worsening over time, also remain hostile to the institution that pursues them.

These pressures converge around a weakened Navy. Glyph, the Syndicate, and other powerful criminal bodies recognize an opportunity to eliminate a major obstacle while its ability to respond has been reduced.

## Preparation Before Open War

For a prolonged portion of the arc, war has not yet been formally declared, but the major participants understand that it is approaching. Tension builds as forces prepare, seek support, and position themselves.

This period contains numerous events and sub-arcs. Its broad direction is the gradual alignment of circumstances toward open war, rather than one immediate declaration following the Syndicate confrontation.

The Navy appeals for assistance. The World Government provides support, and heroes become involved, including [Aidos](../other-characters/aidos.md), the world's #1 most famous hero.

Many nations are unwilling to help. Despite the Navy's previous protection and law-enforcement work, they fear confronting Glyph, the largest nation and empire in the world. The prospect of also facing the global Assassin Syndicate and other criminal powers makes the situation still more dangerous.

## Francesca as Pheme

After her dismissal, Francesca changes her appearance through surgery and becomes publicly known as **Pheme**. This document continues calling her Francesca for continuity with the rest of the repository.

Her change of identity and her underlying motivation are explained in [Identity and Reputation Management](../francesca-badu/psychology/identity-and-reputation-management.md). She wants to establish a reputation as an extraordinary criminal by defeating and killing powerful, recognizable people.

She does not adopt the Navy's or Glyph's wartime objectives. Her activities occur alongside the preparations and fighting, frequently outside the principal war narrative.

### Reuniting With Salore

Francesca encounters **Salore**, the world's fourth-most-wanted criminal, again after their earlier confrontation at the end of TTC.

Salore does not initially recognize her. Her appearance has changed (she perfromed plastic surgery on herself), she uses a different identity, and a substantial interval has passed. The reunion is described approximately as occurring around a year after their first encounter; the complete chronology of the intervening arcs remains to be finalized.

Both are G-rank combatants at this point.

### Joining Hideki's Crew

Francesca and Salore meet [Hideki](../other-characters/hideki.md), the world's most-wanted criminal, and join his crew.

Hideki is a G-rank combatant whose principal magic is light magic. He treats his crew as family: brothers, sisters, and people for whom he feels a parental responsibility. His crew includes many exceptionally dangerous criminals, including individuals among the world's hundred most wanted.

An entire sub-arc follows Francesca and Salore traveling with them. They spend roughly half a year together, becoming familiar members of the group and receiving its familial trust.

Francesca's relationship with Salore also becomes personally significant. Her genuine attachment to him remains distinct from intrinsic compassion for his welfare, as explained in [Attachment and Relationships](../francesca-badu/psychology/attachment-and-relationships.md).

## Open War and the Appointment of Horus

After further intervening developments, the Grand Navy formally declares war on Glyph. Glyph immediately declares war in return.

Glyph, the Assassin Syndicate, and other criminal bodies mobilize against the Navy. The World Government fights alongside the Navy, providing troops and peacekeepers. The conflict includes large land engagements, fleets exchanging fire, and thousands of deaths.

The Navy is losing and enters an increasingly severe crisis.

### Public Objectives and Historical Motives

The Navy publicly seeks to overthrow Glyph's dictatorship and liberate its civilians. Civilian liberation is an actual objective, not merely a statement concealing an entirely unrelated purpose.

Historical hostility also contributes to the eagerness for confrontation. The Navy remembers Glyph's attack on Holy Meekaz and the assassination of its king. Glyph, in turn, regards the Navy as a threat and gains confidence from the support of the Syndicate and other criminal powers.

These motives coexist. The conflict cannot be explained exclusively as humanitarian intervention or exclusively as historical revenge.

### The Second Fleet Admiral

During the crisis, the Board of Military Chiefs meets with its members, including the King Admiral and the four Admirals, and formally appoints [Horus](../other-characters/horus.md) as Fleet Admiral.

Horus is only the second Fleet Admiral in Navy history. The first held the role during a war roughly a century earlier, outside the events directly explored in the main story.

Horus takes command of all four Admirals and unifies their forces for the war.

He is a G-rank combatant and a distant descendant of the king of Holy Meekaz whom Glyph assassinated. The unresolved historical grievance is therefore also personal to him. He is deeply committed to seeing Glyph answer for what happened.

At the same time, he genuinely wants to protect and liberate Glyph's civilians. His personal grievance does not erase that concern.

## Gektor and Glyph's National Hero Team

Gektor leads Glyph's national team of heroes, a party of some of the empire's strongest fighters. He is one of the war's principal protagonists and is established as an upstanding man who wants to do good.

His role on Glyph's side does not make him interchangeable with its dictator. During the war, his team fights Navy forces and saves Glyph's civilians from the dangers of the conflict.

The Navy is targeting the empire's military and leadership, rather than deliberately pursuing civilian deaths. Nevertheless, civilians are killed during the fighting, and Gektor confronts those consequences.

Gektor is normally SSS rank. Under exceptional exertion, his combat performance can reach a level at which he can stand against a low-tier G-rank opponent. He is included in the G-Rank Free For All on that basis; this does not make his ordinary level identical to that of every participant.

## Francesca Defeats a Glyph Army

During the war, a Glyph army encounters Francesca and Hideki's crew while traveling. Francesca defeats that army by herself.

The dictator reacts with fury at the humiliation of an army losing to one person. He decides that Francesca must be killed.

Rather than simply assigning an ordinary mission, he takes the families of the national hero team's members hostage. Gektor's wife and children are among those threatened.

The order is coercive: the heroes must defeat Francesca or their families will be killed.

### The National Hero Team's Defeat

The team tracks Francesca down and fights her. They lose.

The detailed choreography, abilities, and individual exchanges of this battle have not yet been documented. This summary establishes the result and its consequences without supplying invented combat details.

Gektor is the team's only surviving member. He is so severely injured that Francesca believes he is probably dead and leaves him behind.

He barely survives and requires reconstructive surgery and exceptionally advanced magical healing.

The dictator gives him a final chance. The lives of the hostages—including the families of his fallen teammates—still depend on his killing Francesca. Gektor resumes the pursuit under that threat.

## Betrayal of Hideki's Crew

After further intervening sub-arcs, Francesca and Salore identify the crew itself as an opportunity to increase their criminal reputations.

They betray Hideki and slaughter the crew while he is away on a separate mission. The crew numbers roughly a hundred people; the massacre includes highly wanted criminals whose deaths contribute substantially to Francesca's notoriety.

The people they kill had traveled with them and treated them as family.

Hideki returns to find his crew dead and Francesca and Salore gone. His familial attachment turns their betrayal into a devastating personal loss. He pursues both of them to avenge the people he trusted them to live alongside.

Francesca and Salore continue traveling. Approximately a month passes between the massacre and their arrival in the region where the free-for-all develops.

## The Convergence in Glyph's Outer Territories

Several exceptionally powerful individuals arrive in the same broad region near the outer edges of Glyph's territory. The environment is snowy, with ice along the ocean's edge.

**Each participant has an individual reason to be there. Their convergence is the intersection of separate journeys, missions, and prior storylines.** The complete histories of those journeys would require recounting substantial material unrelated to Francesca, so only the immediate reasons needed here are summarized.

Some participants have already been protagonists of their own much earlier arcs. Their presence is not their first introduction to the audience.

### Horus: Betrayal Aboard His Ship

A Rear Admiral serving as one of Horus's most trusted subordinates is secretly a Glyph spy.

While Horus is asleep aboard a Navy ship, the spy attempts to kill him with poison. Believing he has ensured Horus's death, the spy then destroys the ship in an explosion, sacrificing himself for Glyph and killing hundreds of Navy personnel aboard.

Horus survives. His G-rank resilience makes the assassination attempt less conclusive than the spy believes.

He reaches the ice along Glyph's outer coastal territory alone and must make his way through the region after the loss of the ship.

### Baku: Searching for Horus

[Baku](../other-characters/baku.md) is a member of the World Government's governing board and its only G-rank member.

The World Government does not know that the ship's destruction resulted from an internal assassination attempt. A disaster involving the vessel of a G-rank Fleet Admiral could indicate an extraordinarily powerful enemy.

Rather than expose an ordinary rescue force or army to an unknown threat capable of destroying it, Baku volunteers to investigate personally and search for Horus. He travels along the outer territory.

Baku is strongly anti-demi-human. His position within the World Government and the consequences of his later death matter beyond this rescue mission.

### Francesca and Salore: Traveling After the Massacre

Francesca and Salore reach the outer territories while traveling away from the place where they killed Hideki's crew.

They are in the same region, but Francesca is temporarily occupied elsewhere when the other combatants first converge. She is not present for the initial confrontation.

### Hideki: Pursuing the Killers of His Crew

Hideki follows Francesca and Salore to avenge the massacre.

His pursuit brings him into the same region and eventually into contact with Salore before Francesca arrives.

### Gektor: Pursuing Francesca Under Threat

Following his recovery, Gektor tracks Francesca to the outer territories. He still has to kill her to save the families held by the dictator.

He also encounters Sola Sola, a wanted fugitive from Glyph, also a G rank, giving him another target within the developing confrontation. Gektor sincerely believes the empire's accusations against Sola Sola. Those accusations are fabricated, but Gektor does not know that; he believes he is pursuing a genuine criminal.

### Aidos: An Existing Mission in the Region

Aidos has already been operating in and around Glyph's territory as part of a storyline established much earlier in the series.

His own arc ended many arcs and a very large number of chapters before this confrontation. His reasons for being in the region predate the immediate convergence.

He is a G-rank combatant and an ally assisting the Navy. His full earlier journey is not reproduced here.

### Sola Sola: Fleeing Glyph

**Sola Sola** is a mechanic from Glyph, a low-tier G-rank combatant, and a former protagonist whose own arc occurred much earlier.

He is a good person whom the empire nevertheless labels a criminal and pursues. Following the events of his arc, he has been on the run.

His power does not make endless confrontation a sensible or sustainable option. He could defeat substantial forces, but repeated attacks would eventually wear him down, and he does not want to keep fighting the empire's armies.

Before its destruction, Glyph's national hero team would likely have been capable of defeating him.

His escape route brings him through the outer territories to the same region as the other participants.

## The G-Rank Free For All

### Initial Fighting

Some of the converging individuals encounter one another and begin fighting. The disturbance draws the attention of others, bringing more participants into the confrontation.

The fighting eventually moves into an enormous, arena-like cave.

The principal participants before Francesca's arrival are:

| Participant | Immediate involvement |
| --- | --- |
| Horus | Surviving Fleet Admiral, separated from his forces |
| Baku | Searching for Horus; fights alongside him |
| Aidos | Hero already operating in the region |
| Hideki | Seeking revenge on Salore and Francesca |
| Salore | Traveling with Francesca; confronted while she is absent |
| Gektor | Hunting Francesca under threat to the hostages; also targets Sola Sola |
| Sola Sola | Fugitive trying to escape Glyph |

Gektor's exceptional combat state allows him to participate at approximately low-tier G-rank level despite his normal SSS classification.

### Hideki's Proposal and Salore's Death

Hideki interrupts the fighting and argues for a temporary common objective.

Salore is already present, and Francesca could arrive at any time. If those two G-ranks can fight together, the others will face an exceptionally dangerous pair.

Hideki proposes that everyone put their differences aside long enough to kill Salore before Francesca can join him.

The others unite against Salore. Aidos and Horus land the finishing blows, killing him.

Their cooperation is temporary. After Salore's death, the participants resume fighting among themselves.

### Why Aidos Does Not Remain Allied With Baku

Horus and Baku fight together. Aidos would ordinarily be aligned with the Navy and World Government in the wider war, but his relationship with Baku creates a separate conflict.

Aidos objects to Baku's anti-demi-human position and his neglect of demi-human suffering and conflicts. He wants to bring Baku to justice and arrest him, rather than kill him.

Aidos would not ordinarily storm the World Government's headquarters to attack a board member. The existing confrontation creates an opportunity in which he can act against Baku directly.

Consequently, the free-for-all is not simply the Navy and its allies fighting Glyph and criminals. Apart from Horus and Baku's continuing partnership, the participants fight across the broader war's usual alignments.

### Escalation Toward Supreme Arts

The battle continues through an extended sequence of shifting attacks and confrontations.

In an animated presentation, this portion is envisioned as roughly thirty minutes of sustained fighting before Francesca's intervention becomes the focus. This is an approximate audience-facing duration, not a precise measurement of elapsed time within the setting.

The escalation reaches a point where the combatants prepare their Supreme Arts simultaneously.

Supreme Arts impose personal laws within specified jurisdictions through exceptionally precise, practiced requests. Their activation requires coordinated gestures, spoken invocations, and automatic execution of the trained intent. Jurisdictional conflicts, mental strain, and Refusal Writs are explained in [Supreme Arts](../Arms,%20Equipment%20&%20Abilities/magic/supreme-arts.md), rather than repeated here.

This escalation is interrupted by Francesca before it establishes a completed clash. The scene does not supply an outcome for the ordinary arts' competing jurisdictions. Francesca has no Supreme Art at this point; her later invention of a Liberated Supreme Art belongs specifically to her endgame confrontation with Malu.

## Francesca's Arrival and Response to Salore's Death

Francesca arrives while the others are still fighting and initially goes unnoticed.

She finds Salore's decapitated head, kneels, and cradles it in her lap. She looks down at it with a blank expression for several seconds. She is grieving, but she does not cry.

She then leans farther forward until her hair falls around her face like a curtain, screening out the surrounding world. She remains like that briefly before lowering his head and rising.

This is a genuine response to the sudden loss of someone to whom she is attached. It does not overturn the distinction between attachment and compassion described in her psychological documentation.

### Physical Disruption of the Cave

Her expression remains outwardly neutral, but she becomes intensely enraged. Her heart races, her breathing becomes extraordinarily heavy, and her muscles twitch.

The physical effect spreads through the enormous cave. Her breathing disturbs its air so severely that the available air and pressure perceptibly drop. Her muscular activity contributes to an oppressive pressure that feels to the others as though gravity has doubled.

The description concerns the sensation and disturbance experienced by the combatants; it does not establish that she magically changes gravity.

At the moment the others announce their Supreme Arts, the disturbance interrupts their escalation. They freeze and turn toward Francesca, now standing near the entrance beside Salore's remains.

the disruption is produced by her extraordinary physical ability and physiological activity, not a magical aura, a Supreme Art of her own, or a spell.

The other combatants recognize that something profoundly dangerous has entered the fight.

## Francesca Against the Remaining Combatants

The surviving participants unite against Francesca.

At first, their combined force is winning. Even with Francesca operating at the upper end of G-rank capability and intensely enraged, fighting the group is not an effortless victory.

Her expression remains largely neutral. The lack of an overtly furious expression should not be mistaken for emotional calm.

### Aidos Dies From Tarlylox

Aidos is suffering from Tarlylox, the disease described in [The Series Conflict](../the-series-conflict.md).

During the battle, the disease reaches its terminal cellular deterioration. His body fails, and he dies standing upright.

Francesca is about to strike him when she realizes that he is already dead and stops the attack.

**Francesca does not kill Aidos. He dies from the disease during the battle.**

His death removes the strongest fighter opposing her and changes the balance of the confrontation. From that point, the coalition's situation deteriorates as Francesca gradually defeats its remaining members.

### Salore's Weapon Bag and the Conditions of Her Victory

During the continuing fight, Francesca takes [Salore's Weapon Bag](../Arms,%20Equipment%20&%20Abilities/salores-weapon-bag.md) from his body and uses its contents.

The bag is a major reason she wins. Her intelligence lets her grasp unfamiliar weapons' functions exceptionally quickly and devise effective combinations and applications during combat.

Her victory is not attributed to physical ability alone. Without the bag, the fight would probably remain close, but she would likely lose. The arsenal and her ability to exploit it provide the additional advantage necessary to overcome the remaining opponents.

Even with the bag, Francesca barely wins. She suffers severe injuries and emerges from an extremely close confrontation. Her eventual victory and exceptional individual feats should not be interpreted as evidence that she comfortably overpowers the group. The specific injuries, her appearance afterward, and the details of her post-battle condition remain to be documented.

The detailed weapon inventory and established mechanics remain in the bag's own document rather than being repeated here.

### Hideki's Attempted Escape

Hideki transforms into a beam or trail of light through his own light magic and attempts to escape at massively faster-than-light speed.

Francesca catches up and appears ahead of him before he can respond. She kills him immediately.

This is physical movement, not teleportation. Even from Hideki's perspective, despite his own massively faster-than-light reactions, her arrival looks instantaneous.

### Baku and “Checkmate”

Baku is the last surviving opponent.

Earlier during the continuing battle, Francesca finds the [delayed-voice megaphone](../Arms,%20Equipment%20&%20Abilities/salores-weapon-bag.md) in Salore's bag (read weapon bag to understand how megaphone works). She sets its delay and speaks “Checkmate” into it. The word is inaudible at the moment she records it.

She continues fighting for several dozen minutes.

At the calculated final moment, she kills Baku from a distance using the bag's unstoppable-projectile sniper rifle. He is at the location where she originally spoke.

The delayed recording plays from that location at precisely the moment of his death, making “Checkmate” the final word he hears.

The megaphone does not predict the future. Francesca supplies the calculations and timing; the item supplies delayed, location-anchored playback.

### Outcome

All the combatants who oppose Francesca in this confrontation die. Horus, Gektor, Sola Sola, Hideki, and Baku are among those she kills. Aidos dies from Tarlylox, and Salore has already been killed by the temporary alliance before her intervention.

The individual death sequences not described above remain undocumented rather than being filled in with invented attacks.

The gathering represents roughly half of the world's contemporary G-rank-level combatants, not every G-rank alive. Other extraordinarily powerful individuals are elsewhere and do not participate.

## Consequences and Limits of This Account

The deaths remove major military, political, and personal actors and change the wider balance of power. Nevertheless, this single confrontation does not settle the Navy–Glyph war. Its full resolution and numerous other decisive wartime events are not documented here.

Francesca does not fight the group to win the war for either side. Her reputation-seeking activities and, in this confrontation, her intense response to Salore's death intersect with the war without becoming an ideological commitment to its objectives.

### Baku and the World Government's Political Balance

Before his death, the World Government's leadership is divided between pro-demi-human and anti-demi-human members.

Baku is one of its anti-demi-human leaders. His death removes a vote and a powerful advocate from that side, leaving a pro-demi-human majority.

This shift helps explain the World Government's later official pro-demi-human position and its opposition to Leon's regime. It belongs to the chain of consequences explored in [Post–G-Rank Free For All](./post-g-rank-free-for-all.md).

The broader consequences of Francesca's victories also create an opportunity for Leon to organize his movement. Those later developments are separate from Francesca's immediate reasons for fighting here.

### The Navy's Defeat and Effective Collapse

Horus's death does not immediately end the war. However, it removes the Fleet Admiral who had unified the four Admirals and their forces at a time when the Grand Navy is already losing badly.

The loss severely damages morale. Over the following months, the absence of unified command, continuing military defeats, and growing internal divisions progressively undermine the institution.

Some personnel quit or desert. Some surviving divisions compete for control, and individuals attempt to secure the position of the next Fleet Admiral. Others continue fighting, are killed, or are captured and imprisoned.

These developments do not follow a single coordinated decision. Different parts of the Navy respond differently as its leadership and cohesion deteriorate.

The Grand Navy ultimately loses the war. It is not formally dissolved, but within the following months it effectively ceases to function as a unified worldwide institution.

Thousands upon thousands of surviving personnel, deserters, and fragmented divisions remain scattered across different regions. Some continue competing for authority, while others abandon the conflict and flee.

Horus's death is therefore a major contributing event in the Navy's fall, rather than an instantaneous end to the organization. The collapse develops through the interaction of lost leadership, deteriorating morale, internal struggles, and an already disastrous military situation.
