# Leon

The leader of [Leon's Regime](../../world-powers/anti-demi-human-international-coalition.md)

## Overview and First Appearance

**Leon** is the principal organizer and ideological leader of the international anti-demi-human movement that eventually develops into a major military and political coalition.

He is first introduced in the **[Post–G-Rank Free For All arc](../../arcs/post-g-rank-free-for-all.md)**, following Francesca Badu's participation in the G-Rank Free For All. The Post G Rank document is significant to understanding Leon as well so it should be read.

Leon's introduction occurs at this point because Francesca's actions during the preceding arc create the circumstances that convince him that his political and ideological objectives may finally be achievable.

Although Leon is closely associated with Francesca, publicly known as **Pheme**, he is the individual primarily responsible for organizing and directing the anti-demi-human coalition. Francesca does not share his ideological commitment and generally takes little interest in the movement's actual objectives.

Leon is documented in this repository primarily because his actions, beliefs, and relationship with Francesca are necessary to understand her later reputation, public role, and involvement in the final conflicts of the story.

## Background: The Demi-Human Conflict

The world contains humans and demi-humans, with demi-humans including various populations possessing characteristics that distinguish them from ordinary humans.

Demi-humans are affected by an incurable disease that can spread to other individuals through proximity. The disease is capable of killing demi-humans and humans alike. Humans who contract it do not themselves transmit it to others.

No known cure or effective treatment exists.

The existence of this disease creates an ongoing political, social, and humanitarian conflict.

Different nations, organizations, and individuals hold widely varying positions concerning demi-humans and the appropriate response to the danger posed by the disease.

These positions exist along a broad spectrum.

Some groups advocate protecting demi-humans and preserving their rights and ability to participate in society. Others support measures such as separation, exclusion, or restrictions intended to limit the transmission of the disease.

More extreme groups advocate the extermination of demi-humans.

The motivations behind these positions also differ. Some individuals are genuinely concerned about the consequences of the disease, while others are motivated partly or primarily by prejudice and use the disease as a justification for their hostility.

These differences are significant. Not every individual or organization opposed to the continued integration of demi-humans supports extermination, and not every supporter of extermination arrives at that position for the same reasons.

Before Francesca's involvement in the G-Rank Free For All, the international political situation is effectively a stalemate.

Powerful nations, organizations, and individuals support opposing positions. Attempts by either side to impose its preferred policies risk provoking military retaliation and potentially catastrophic conflict.

Consequently, many groups are unwilling or unable to take decisive action, despite their strongly held beliefs.

The underlying conflict remains unresolved and continues to intensify.

## Leon's Ideology

Leon genuinely believes that the continued existence of demi-humans represents an existential threat to humanity.

From his perspective, allowing the disease to continue spreading will eventually result in the deaths of humanity and the demi-human population alike.

He believes that eliminating demi-humans, although it would involve enormous loss of life, would prevent an even greater catastrophe by allowing humanity to survive.

His reasoning can be illustrated by a simplified hypothetical comparison:

- If demi-humans are allowed to continue existing, the disease eventually causes the deaths of everyone.
- If demi-humans are exterminated, an enormous number of people die, but the remaining human population survives.

This comparison represents Leon's understanding of the situation. It is **not** a statement of established mortality statistics, a verified prediction, or an objective demonstration that these are the only possible outcomes.

Leon nonetheless regards extermination as the necessary course of action.

He does not merely regard it as a politically advantageous policy. He believes it is morally justified and, more strongly, that protecting humanity through the elimination of demi-humans is a moral obligation.

He considers failing to act equivalent to allowing a preventable catastrophe to occur.

His conviction is sincere. His ideology is not simply a public justification for prejudice, personal ambition, or an underlying desire to inflict suffering.

However, his sincerity does not make his conclusions objectively correct.

Leon's certainty about the future consequences of the disease and the necessity of extermination reflects his own convictions, rather than a conclusively established fact about the fictional world.

## Psychological State Before His Introduction

Before the G-Rank Free For All, Leon experiences considerable despair and frustration.

He believes that humanity is approaching an eventual catastrophe, yet he lacks the power and political influence necessary to prevent what he considers inevitable.

Even though he is deeply committed to his beliefs, he understands that attempting to initiate a campaign against demi-humans would provoke resistance from powerful nations and individuals.

He cannot simply begin an extermination campaign without risking overwhelming retaliation.

This produces a persistent sense of helplessness.

From his perspective, he is watching the world approach extinction while being unable to meaningfully intervene.

His distress is genuine. He believes that inaction will lead to humanity's destruction, and his inability to change the situation affects him emotionally.

These beliefs and circumstances exist before Francesca's involvement in the events that eventually lead to his introduction.

## The G-Rank Free For All and Leon's Decision to Act

During the G-Rank Free For All, Francesca defeats and kills numerous extraordinarily powerful combatants.

Several of those killed are demi-humans.

However, Francesca's decisions are not motivated by hostility toward demi-humans or support for Leon's ideology.

Her objective is to improve her reputation by defeating exceptionally powerful opponents.

The identities, political affiliations, and species of those opponents are not the primary reasons she fights them.

Nevertheless, her actions have enormous political consequences.

Some of the individuals she kills would otherwise have represented major obstacles to an anti-demi-human military campaign.

Her actions also affect the political balance of the World Government, including through the death of an anti-demi-human G-rank individual associated with its leadership.

When Leon hears about Francesca's victories, he recognizes that the existing balance of power has changed.

Individuals who previously made an extermination campaign appear militarily impossible are now dead.

For the first time, Leon believes that his objective may be achievable.

This realization marks a turning point in his life.

Rather than remaining powerless, he decides to act.

He begins approaching nations, organizations, influential individuals, and other groups that might support a coordinated anti-demi-human movement.

His efforts eventually result in the formation of a large international coalition.

Francesca's actions serve as an unintended catalyst for Leon's rise. She does not deliberately create his ideology, organize his movement, or plan the geopolitical consequences of her victories.

## Leadership and Ruthlessness

Leon becomes the principal organizer and leader of his movement.

He is deeply committed to its objectives and willing to employ extreme measures in pursuit of them.

He is capable of killing people without significant hesitation when he believes doing so is necessary to advance his goals.

His willingness to commit violence does not require him to enjoy suffering or killing.

Rather, he regards the suffering and deaths resulting from his policies as acceptable or necessary costs of preventing what he believes would be humanity's eventual extinction.

This distinction is important to his characterization.

Leon is not merely pretending to believe in his cause, nor is he necessarily seeking violence for its own sake.

He genuinely believes that what he is doing is necessary.

However, that conviction also makes him extremely dangerous. His belief that he is preventing a greater catastrophe allows him to justify atrocities without abandoning his self-understanding as someone acting for humanity's survival.

The coalition he organizes contains groups whose motivations are not necessarily identical to his own.

Some share his belief that extermination is necessary for survival. Others may have different ideological, political, or prejudicial reasons for supporting the movement.

Their cooperation does not mean they all share precisely the same beliefs.

## Leon's Perception of Francesca

Leon becomes deeply infatuated with Francesca.

His attachment includes both intense personal attraction and extraordinary admiration for her perceived role in humanity's future.

He regards her as exceptionally beautiful, powerful, intelligent, and worthy of reverence.

He also interprets her victories during the G-Rank Free For All as evidence that she is a savior capable of liberating humanity from the threat he believes demi-humans represent.

Leon elevates Francesca into a figure of near-divine importance.

He publicly celebrates her as humanity's savior and presents her as the extraordinary individual who will guide the movement toward its ultimate objective.

His admiration is sincere.

Francesca is not merely a convenient political symbol that Leon exploits without personal attachment. He genuinely idolizes her and is romantically infatuated with her.

However, Leon's understanding of Francesca is substantially distorted by his own beliefs and desires.

He interprets her strength, reputation, and actions through the framework of his ideology.

He assumes that the woman he worships must share his convictions, despite lacking adequate evidence for this conclusion.

In reality, Francesca's motivations are largely unrelated to Leon's cause.

## Francesca's Actual Relationship to the Movement

Francesca is generally indifferent to the ideological conflict between humans and demi-humans.

She does not have Leon's desire to exterminate demi-humans, nor does she regard the movement's objectives as a personal moral obligation.

However, she enjoys the extraordinary admiration and recognition associated with her position.

Leon's movement praises her beauty, strength, intelligence, and supposed divine significance.

This attention directly satisfies her desire to be recognized as an exceptional individual.

Consequently, she willingly accepts her position as the movement's symbolic figurehead.

Her acceptance does not mean that she privately shares its ideology.

Leon remains the primary organizer and political leader, while Francesca generally contributes little to the movement's planning and administration.

She is largely content to enjoy the admiration she receives while observing events without substantial personal involvement.

Nevertheless, her lack of ideological commitment does not mean she is unaware of the movement's activities or that her acceptance of its symbolic leadership has no consequences.

Her public association with the movement helps reinforce the importance Leon and his followers assign to her.

## The Conversation About Moral Obligation

At a later point, Leon directly asks Francesca whether she agrees that purging demi-humans is a moral obligation for the benefit of human society.

Leon expects an answer consistent with the beliefs he attributes to her.

Francesca considers the question for approximately one second and responds negatively.

Her answer is simply **"No."**

She does not provide Leon with an extended explanation of her philosophical reasoning.

From Francesca's perspective, the question is not particularly important. Her extraordinary cognitive capabilities allow her to conduct a complex philosophical analysis with very little effort, and she has little interest in continuing the discussion.

Her complete internal reasoning is documented separately in [Francesca's Opinion on Morality](../../francesca-badu/psychology/opinions-on-morals.md).

The audience does not witness that internal analysis. In the story, the exchange is brief.

For Leon, however, the answer is emotionally devastating.

He has elevated Francesca into an almost divine figure and convinced himself that she supports his cause.

Her direct disagreement threatens his understanding of someone he deeply admires and worships.

He becomes deeply distressed for several weeks.

Eventually, rather than accepting that Francesca genuinely disagrees with him, Leon rationalizes her response.

He convinces himself that she must have had some deeper reason for answering negatively.

He assumes that her true intentions are beyond his present understanding and that he will eventually discover the explanation.

In doing so, he effectively dismisses the plain meaning of her answer.

He continues pursuing his ideology while believing that his actions remain consistent with what Francesca ultimately wants.

This is not the result of Francesca secretly explaining another position to him.

Leon constructs this interpretation himself.

## Idealization and Self-Deception

One of Leon's defining psychological characteristics is his tendency to idealize Francesca and interpret contradictory information in ways that preserve his existing beliefs about her.

He considers her extraordinarily intelligent and superior to himself.

Consequently, when her behavior conflicts with his expectations, he can explain the contradiction by assuming that she possesses some deeper understanding he has not yet attained.

This allows him to preserve both his ideological convictions and his worship of Francesca.

His reaction demonstrates that he is not necessarily responding to Francesca as she actually exists.

Instead, his devotion is strongly influenced by an idealized conception of who she is and what she supposedly represents.

His attachment to her remains genuine, but his understanding of her motivations is deeply inaccurate.

The contrast with Francesca is significant.

Francesca enjoys receiving admiration from Leon and his followers, but she does not need to share their beliefs or deliberately construct the interpretations Leon assigns to her.

Much of Leon's misunderstanding arises from his own emotional investment and motivated reasoning.

## Public Perception and the World Government

Leon's movement regards Francesca as a savior and guiding figure.

The opposing World Government regards her as an extraordinarily dangerous enemy associated with the anti-demi-human coalition.

The World Government identifies her as **the Enemy of Humanity**, while Leon's movement glorifies her as **the Savior**.

Both sides assign Francesca major symbolic significance, but neither properly understands her personal motivations.

Leon's movement interprets her extraordinary power as evidence of ideological legitimacy.

The World Government regards her as a central threat associated with the opposing coalition.

Francesca herself remains primarily concerned with her own reputation and the admiration she receives.

The difference between these perspectives is essential to understanding her eventual role in the conflict.

## Leon's Later Fate

During the later stages of the worldwide conflict, Leon is assassinated.

His death does not immediately end the war.

The conflict continues.

## Narrative Relevance to Francesca

Leon's importance to Francesca's characterization lies primarily in the difference between the role he assigns to her and her actual motivations.

Leon regards Francesca as humanity's savior, an extraordinary woman whose victories and power validate his cause.

Francesca views the same relationship primarily as a source of admiration and personal recognition.

Leon is deeply emotionally and ideologically invested in their shared public association.

Francesca enjoys the benefits of that association without sharing his convictions.

His actions contribute to transforming Francesca from an extraordinarily powerful individual pursuing personal recognition into a globally significant political symbol and, eventually, a principal target of the opposing coalition.

## Private Emotional Responses and Devotion to Francesca

Leon occasionally experiences intense emotional responses when reflecting privately on his life, accomplishments, and the opportunity he believes Francesca has given him.

These moments do not necessarily occur during important events, battles, or political developments.

They can happen during entirely ordinary circumstances, such as when Leon is sitting alone in his office, resting, or simply thinking without doing anything in particular.

During these moments, Leon may begin reflecting on how dramatically his life has changed.

Before the G-Rank Free For All, he regarded himself as someone who had accomplished practically nothing meaningful.

He believed humanity was approaching extinction, yet he lacked the power to do anything about it.

His inability to act had caused him considerable despair, frustration, and feelings of personal worthlessness.

Now, however, he is actually making a difference in the world.

He has begun building an increasingly powerful movement, gathering supporters, and taking actions that he believes are necessary to prevent humanity's extinction.

What once seemed completely impossible has become something he can actively pursue.

Sometimes, simply realizing this is enough to overwhelm him emotionally.

### Silent Tears

During these private moments of reflection, tears may begin streaming down Leon's face.

Rather, they frequently result from Leon being deeply moved by the realization that he is finally accomplishing something he considers meaningful.

He reflects on the fact that, after spending so much of his life feeling powerless and useless, he now has an opportunity to do something of extraordinary importance.

He genuinely believes he is contributing to humanity's survival.

The realization that he is no longer completely helpless affects him profoundly.

### Francesca's Importance to Him

These reflections frequently lead Leon to think about Francesca.

He believes that without her actions during the G-Rank Free For All, he might never have found an opportunity to pursue his objective.

From his perspective, Francesca is the reason his current life is possible.

She represents the turning point between his former helplessness and his newfound sense of purpose.

Leon regards this opportunity as an extraordinary gift, almost as though it had been sent from heaven.

Although Francesca never intended to provide him with such an opportunity, Leon attaches immense personal significance to her actions.

His admiration for her extends far beyond recognizing her extraordinary power, intelligence, and beauty.

She occupies an enormously important place in his emotional life.

Leon is deeply infatuated with her, idealizes her, and regards her as an almost divine figure responsible for making humanity's supposed salvation possible.

When reflecting on Francesca, he may become overwhelmed by gratitude, admiration, relief, and the realization that she has indirectly transformed his existence.

He believes that she has given him the opportunity to make his life meaningful.

In this sense, Francesca becomes inseparable from Leon's understanding of his own purpose.

### Emotional Significance

These moments demonstrate that Leon's commitment to his ideology is not merely an intellectual conviction or a political objective.

It is also deeply connected to his personal identity, emotional well-being, and sense of self-worth.

He genuinely believes that he has finally found a reason to live meaningfully.

Even when he is alone, without anyone watching him or praising his accomplishments, this realization can move him to tears.

His emotional response is therefore not a performance intended to impress his followers or reinforce his public image.

It is a genuine private reaction.

**For Leon, Francesca is not merely someone he admires. She is the person whose actions made it possible for him to escape what he regarded as a meaningless, powerless existence and dedicate himself to what he believes is humanity's salvation.**

**Leon is not simply a subordinate acting under Francesca's direction. He is an independent ideological actor who constructs a movement around an idealized interpretation of someone whose actual priorities are fundamentally different from his own.**

## Leon's Understanding of His Opponents and His Desire for Peace

The World Leon Wants to Change

An important aspect of Leon's motivation is his desire to end the prolonged suffering, instability, and conflict surrounding the relationship between humans and demi-humans.

For decades, the world has experienced severe tensions between the two populations. Discrimination, persecution, violent confrontations, and occasional genocides have contributed to an increasingly unstable international environment.

Different nations, organizations, and populations hold opposing positions regarding the demi-human disease and how society should respond to it.

These disagreements have repeatedly produced violence and political instability. The international situation has become so tense that the possibility of a major worldwide war remains a constant concern.

For many people, this is a world characterized by fear, uncertainty, hostility, and suffering rather than lasting peace.

Leon genuinely does not want this situation to continue.

He wants humanity to have a future free from the threat of extinction, the ongoing conflict surrounding the disease, and the widespread fear and suffering associated with it.

From his perspective, the continued existence of demi-humans and the incurable disease they transmit makes such a future impossible.

He therefore believes that exterminating demi-humans, despite the enormous suffering and loss of life it would cause, is necessary to prevent an even greater catastrophe and ultimately create a peaceful future for humanity.

This is Leon's sincere conviction, not an objectively established conclusion about the only possible solution.

His Understanding of Those Who Oppose Him

Leon does not inherently regard the people opposing his extermination campaign as evil or malicious.

He understands that many of them are motivated by compassion, concern for innocent lives, and a genuine desire to prevent suffering.

He recognizes these motivations because he shares the underlying desire to protect people from suffering.

Leon himself does not enjoy inflicting suffering. He does not believe that individual demi-humans necessarily deserve to die, nor does he require personal hatred toward them to justify his actions.

He nevertheless believes that allowing demi-humans to continue existing will eventually result in humanity's extinction.

Consequently, he interprets the suffering caused by his extermination campaign as a terrible but necessary sacrifice to prevent what he believes would be an incomparably greater catastrophe.

This distinction is important to understanding his attitude toward his enemies.

Leon does not necessarily blame them for resisting him.

He understands why individuals who witness the extermination of innocent people would attempt to intervene. He recognizes that they are acting according to their own understanding of what is right and necessary.

However, he believes their understanding of the larger situation is mistaken.

From his perspective, they are attempting to prevent immediate suffering without recognizing the eventual catastrophe that he believes their resistance will permit.

"Why Can't They Understand Me?"

One of Leon's recurring emotional struggles is his inability to make others understand what he believes he is trying to accomplish.

His frustration does not primarily arise from believing that his opponents are evil or that they deliberately want humanity to become extinct.

Instead, he struggles with the belief that people who also want to prevent suffering cannot recognize what he considers the necessary means of preventing humanity's extinction.

His underlying question is not simply why people oppose him, but why they cannot understand his reasoning.

He believes that if they understood the future he fears and the consequences he believes inaction will produce, they might recognize the necessity of his campaign.

He does not necessarily expect them to agree with him, and he understands their reasons for resisting. Nevertheless, their continued opposition can cause him intense frustration, desperation, and emotional distress.

To Leon, every delay represents additional time during which humanity remains threatened by the disease, while the ongoing conflict continues to produce suffering.

He believes that abandoning his campaign simply because others oppose it would mean allowing the catastrophe he is trying to prevent.

Consequently, he remains determined to continue even when confronted by widespread resistance.

He imagines a future in which humanity no longer has to fear the demi-human disease, where the conflicts surrounding it have ended, and where future generations can live in peace.

he believes that temporary suffering on an enormous scale is necessary to prevent even greater suffering and humanity's eventual extinction.

## Leon's Speech

This is the famous speech by Leon thenight before the start of his full campaign on the world against all Demi Humans, the next day, is effectivly officially the day the world war starts, and this is also the speech where Leon immortalizes Pheme/Francesca as the saviour of humanity (from his POV, and everybody else who follows his regime):

"Your Majesties. Honored leaders. Citizens of every nation.

"Look around you.

"The banners gathered here today, kings and rulers who stand among us. The countless men and women who have journeyed from the farthest reaches of this world to be present at this hour.

"How many of these nations have fought against one another? How many of these rulers have spent their lives divided by borders, by interests, by ancient rivalries?

"For the first time, powers that once stood in opposition have set aside their differences for a purpose greater than any kingdom, greater than any nation, greater than any one of us.

"The survival of humanity."

"For too long, our world has lived beneath the shadow of a terrible affliction.

"We have seen its consequences. We have buried its victims. We have witnessed the suffering of families who could do nothing but watch as those they loved succumbed to Tarlylox.

"And for too long, the nations of this world have remained divided over what must be done.

"Year after year, we have debated.

"Year after year, we have hesitated.

"Year after year, we have watched the danger endure.

"And all the while, time has continued its march.

"My friends, I ask you this: how much longer shall we wait?

"How many more generations must inherit the burden of our indecision?

"There are those who will look upon the suffering it brings and ask how any human being could willingly permit such a thing.

"I have asked myself that question.

"And I will not stand before you today and tell you that the road ahead is without sorrow.

"It is not.

"I will not tell you that the decisions we have made are gentle, or that their consequences will be easy to bear.

"They will not.

"But I believe there is something more terrible still.

"To possess the means to save humanity, and to surrender that opportunity because we lacked the will to act."

"I stand before you today, and I tell you:

"That helplessness has come to an end!"

"..."

"As I stand here, looking upon this extraordinary assembly, I am reminded of something.

"Not long ago, a gathering such as this would have been inconceivable.

"The powers that opposed us seemed too great to overcome. The path before us was closed, and for many of us, hope had become a distant memory.

"I confess to you that there was a time when I believed I would never live to see this day.

"But then..."

"Then, the heavens bestowed upon this world a miracle.

"As though the gates of heaven themselves had opened, and an angel had descended upon our world.

"Someone unlike any who had come before.

"Whose extraordinary gifts would make possible what generations of men had believed impossible.

"And through her, the path before us was opened."

"Tell me."

"WHO IS THE MOST BEAUTIFUL?"

"WHO IS THE MOST INTELLIGENT?"

"WHO IS THE MOST POWERFUL?"

"AND MOST IMPORTANTLY, WHO IS THE SAVIOR GOD HIMSELF SENT DOWN FROM HEAVEN TO RESCUE HUMANITY?"

"Of course, there is only one answer."

"Pheme."
