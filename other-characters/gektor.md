# Gektor

## Overview

**Gektor** leads the [Glyph Empire's](../world-powers/glyph-empire.md) national hero team, a party of some of its strongest fighters. He is one of the principal protagonists followed during [Glyph vs the Grand Navy](../arcs/glyph-vs-navy.md).

## Character and Allegiance

Gektor is an upstanding man who wants to do good. During the war, he fights Navy forces and protects Glyph's civilians from the conflict's consequences. His service to the empire does not make his motives identical to the dictator's.

He has a wife and children.

## Combat Ability

Gektor is normally SSS rank. Under exceptional exertion, he can fight at approximately low-tier G-rank level. His inclusion in the G-Rank Free For All reflects that exceptional performance rather than an assumption that his ordinary ability equals every G-rank's.

## Coercion and Pursuit of Francesca

After Francesca humiliates the dictator by defeating a Glyph army alone, he takes the national heroes' families hostage and orders the team to kill her.

The team loses. Gektor alone survives, suffering injuries that require reconstructive surgery and exceptional magical healing. Francesca leaves him believing he is probably dead.

The dictator gives him one final chance to kill her, with all the hostages' lives still at stake. Gektor tracks Francesca to Glyph's outer territory and becomes involved in the free-for-all. He also targets Sola Sola because he sincerely believes the empire's accusations against him. The accusations are false, but Gektor does not know that and believes he is pursuing a genuine criminal.

Gektor ultimately dies fighting Francesca in the later G-Rank Free For All. The hostages' eventual fate remains undocumented. His first battle, including the gravity-field attempt and his final stand, is recorded below.

For the events, see [Gektor and the National Hero Team](../arcs/glyph-vs-navy.md).

## First Battle With Francesca

### Scope and Stakes

This is the national hero team's **first encounter with Francesca**, during the [Glyph war](../arcs/glyph-vs-navy.md). It is separate from Gektor's later participation in the G-Rank Free For All.

The dictator has taken the team members' families hostage and ordered them to kill Francesca (currently known as Pheme, she's not called Francesca, it's just that the documentation calls her Francesca all the time for simplicity, in the actual story, currently she is known as "Pheme", read Francesca's folder to understand why she is called Pheme). Gektor's wife and children are among the hostages.

The sequence below records the established mechanics and presentation of the first fight. Some team members' names and individual finishing blows remain unspecified. Gektor survives this encounter despite Francesca believing him probably dead.

### Team Composition and Method

The national hero team is a highly coordinated guerrilla fighting force. Before this encounter, it has an undefeated record and often defeats opponents through sensory deception and psychological pressure without needing a straightforward physical contest.

Its members include:

| Member | Established role |
| --- | --- |
| Gektor | Fire conjurer and team leader |
| Ink mage | Sticky ink, controlled clones, and concealment |
| Sound/resonance mage | Suppression, storage, and release of sound and vibration |
| Vector mage | Placed directional points that redirect movement passing through them |
| Combat mage | Direct magical attacks and a Supreme Art |
| Gravity mage | Gravity manipulation; the final sequence is not yet fully documented |
| Light-refraction mage | Placed planes that redirect light to conceal real things and present misleading images |

The light-refraction mage is not a conventional light-attack specialist firing beams and blasts. His technique manipulates the light reaching observers through placed planes.

They have names but we are not naming them for simplicity.

### Francesca's Starting Position

Francesca is not initially exerting herself enough to end the encounter immediately. Her difficulties do not establish that the team is collectively stronger than she is. Had she committed her full effort from the outset, she could have killed them before their methods developed into the same prolonged challenge.

She did not seek out this fight: the team ambushes her to fulfill the dictator's order. Although highly ranked, its members are not correspondingly famous. Defeating them does not necessarily promise the recognizable feat or bounty-related reputational benefit she seeks. High combat rank and public fame are separate, and she does not initially consider the encounter worth her full effort.

Nevertheless, their coordination creates real problems. Her low initial investment gives them time to land attacks while she is still learning how their techniques work.

At this point she has not yet developed the focused ranged impact and sound techniques she acquires during this fight.

She wears the [Eyes of Epiphany](../Arms%2C%20Equipment%20%26%20Abilities/eyes-of-epiphany.md), glasses that let her perceive magic. Their complete origin, capabilities, and limitations will be explained separately; that reference has not yet been supplied here.

The team does not know that her glasses allow this perception. Its members build their tactics around the assumption that she cannot see the magical structures they are using (people don't see magic / can't see magic, mages who are skilled can sense it, but not physically see it. The Eyes actually lets you see them).

### Why Her Predictions Initially Fail

Francesca's predictive calculations, referred to here as **Laplace**, require usable information and an understanding of the processes affecting the battlefield. They do not automatically provide the rules of unfamiliar magic.

Several techniques undermine those conditions at once.

The light-refraction mage conceals real people and presents images of things that are not physically there. The sound mage suppresses movement sounds that would otherwise supply another source of information. Ink creates visual clutter and threatens to cover her glasses. Vector points redirect attacks after she has correctly dodged their initial trajectories.

Gektor and the combat mage exploit those openings with direct attacks.

Francesca can predict an incoming blast and avoid it, yet still be hit when the vector technique redirects it back toward her. Her original calculation does not include that unfamiliar operation because she has never encountered it.

She is struck repeatedly early in the fight, including by magical blasts, Gektor's attacks, and ink. These hits result from unfamiliar mechanics, distorted sensory information, and her incomplete commitment to the fight, rather than an absence of the ability to perform accurate calculations.

### Protecting the Eyes of Epiphany

The ink adheres to what it hits. If it covers her glasses, it can obstruct the magic perception on which her developing analysis depends.

Francesca therefore has to avoid ink carefully while responding to the other attacks.

The glasses provide evidence rather than automatic explanations. She can see a magical structure without already understanding its triggers, limits, or tactical purpose.

Similarly, she can perceive the magic manipulating light, but she does not directly see every redirected light path as a complete, solved diagram. Source positions and orientations help her distinguish some false images from real objects, but her initial interpretation is neither practiced nor consistently accurate enough to defeat all the deception.

### The Vector Loop

The vector mage places directional points, comparable to invisible arrows. Something passing through a point is redirected along its assigned direction.

These points can keep Francesca away from the mage and send already-dodged attacks back toward her. Seeing a point does not mean she can strike or remove it.

At one stage, a square arrangement traps her in a repeated loop. Each point sends her toward the next. She can perceive the points through the glasses, but cannot simply hit them or escape their redirection.

Eventually, the point that began the loop ceases functioning. Its failure breaks the sequence and throws her into a wall across the battlefield.

This gives her a clue. She remembers which point received the initial load and later tests the technique using different forces and interactions.

She concludes that the points have finite redirection limits. Their operation involves an accumulated count of redirected things or groups, with some exclusions, and sufficiently high applied force can exceed their capacity. The repeated loading in the loop eventually exhausts or exceeds the first point's limit.

The exact counting rules, excluded categories, and numerical thresholds are not specified here. The important discovery is that the apparent barrier is neither unlimited nor permanent.

### First Death: The Ink Mage

The ink mage surrounds Francesca with hundreds of ink clones, attempting to overwhelm her.

He also hides himself within ink. However, actively controlling the clones requires magical connections back to the caster.

Francesca can see those connections. Whatever visual concealment surrounds him, the lines converge on his real location.

She stamps the ground hard enough to throw hundreds of pieces of stone and debris into the air. Within roughly a second, she moves among them and individually flicks the fragments in all directions, destroying the surrounding clones.

She follows the visible magical control connections to the hidden caster, reaches him before he can react, and drives her fist through his heart.

He is the first team member killed.

### Learning From the Sound Network

The sound mage has distributed magical strings and containers across the battlefield. They absorb sound waves and vibrations, suppressing ordinary movement sounds while accumulating material for later attacks.

Francesca sees the network through her glasses. Apart from its caster, the other combatants do not share that precise view of its structures.

The releases are dangerous. A direct blast can cause severe internal harm, including ruptured organs. During the fight, Francesca suffers a ruptured eardrum and loses hearing in one ear.

She observes that the network has limited storage. While a container can continue absorbing, nearby movement is suppressed. When it fills, ordinary sound becomes audible again. The mage releases stored energy, and the suppression resumes once capacity is available.

Those changes provide a repeated, observable cycle from which she can infer the limit.

#### Concentrating Physical Impacts

Francesca already knows that her strikes generate pressure, sound, and impacts. Earlier fights, including her confrontation with Salore, produce such effects. She has not previously focused on concentrating them into a controlled ranged attack.

The sound network demonstrates how to do so.

She studies the timing, direction, and pattern of its releases. An initial blast spreads, then later releases overlap it at precisely timed angles until the sequence forms a concentrated attack.

She reproduces the useful physical pattern with her own force. She is not learning to cast the mage's spell or requesting mana.

She experiments while continuing to fight. Over approximately a minute, her execution improves as she refines the timing and concentration.

**She has already developed usable concentrated impacts before killing the vector mage.** Her later aerial sequence further applies those techniques; it is not their first appearance.

Basically, punching and striking so hard that the pressure and sound and impacts fly like a range attack so powerful it can destroy things it hits or slice right through them, and they're also invisible too. 

### Mapping and Killing the Vector Mage

Francesca sends a physical impact wave through the battlefield.

When the wave passes a vector point, **the wave is redirected**. The redirected wave then carries dust, residual ink, and other particles along its new path.

The particles are evidence of the wave's changed motion. This is not a statement that the vector point directly redirects those particles.

By comparing their actual movement with her predicted movement without redirection, she determines where points are placed and the directions in which they face.

She then pressures the mage psychologically and tactically. Her chosen movements force him to reposition points, removing protection from their previous locations and creating openings.

When she approaches, he initially saves himself by recalling the points to defend his own body and repel her.

Francesca has prepared for that response. She launches concentrated impacts from calculated positions and at calculated times so that they will reach him later.

She pressures Gektor, forcing the vector mage to shift the protective points away from himself to intervene around Gektor and redirect the approaching danger.

At the predicted instant his personal protection moves, the already-traveling impacts arrive. They crush and kill the vector mage.

The delayed result is produced through positioning, travel time, and prediction, not by an unexplained magical delay effect.

### Overloading the Sound Network

After the vector mage is dead, Francesca jumps high above the battlefield.

From that position, she rapidly punches downward, sending repeated pressure and impact waves across the field. In the intended animated presentation, her arms and fists become a blur.

She overloads the sound network until its containers and strings burst. Their accumulated sound is released across the team, stunning its members.

Her distance above the field prevents the burst from affecting her meaningfully. That position matters because she has already experienced the danger of the network's attacks and damage to her hearing.

### Distinguishing Bodies From Images

Francesca descends quickly, producing a crater on landing, and sends further waves through the surroundings.

Laplace predicts how those waves should interact with terrain, debris, buildings, trees, and bodies. She compares the expected interactions and returning sounds with what actually happens by calculating (like a LOCALIZED laplace demon https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplace%27s_demon)

A visible person who produces no corresponding physical response can be a false image. A physical response from apparently empty space can reveal an invisible body.

This allows her to locate the concealed light-refraction mage.

His placed planes do **not** require continuous magical control lines back to him. Unlike the ink clones, they cannot simply be traced to their caster through such a connection. This is why she needs the physical test instead of repeating her solution to the ink mage.

### The Light-Refraction Mage's Fatal Injury

Francesca moves immediately in front of the hidden mage, then pauses.

She looks directly toward where she calculates his eyes are. He realizes that she is standing before him and looking at him despite his concealment.

His refraction drops, revealing an expression just beginning to turn to panic.

Rather than attacking him with a full-force punch, Francesca places a hand against his chest and forcefully shoves him backward. The effort is small by her standards, closer to an exaggerated playful push than a committed strike.

He is physically fragile by comparison. The shove throws him across the battlefield into the gravity mage and inflicts fatal injuries, including catastrophic damage to his spine and vertebrae, broken ribs injuring his lungs, and brain injury.

**He is not killed instantaneously.** He survives long enough to deliver the dying warning described below. The word “curse” in the earlier description refers to that anguished threat, not an established supernatural effect.

Francesca laughs with excitement at the success of her new method. Her delight in this sequence is connected to solving the sensory problem and developing her techniques; it need not be interpreted as pleasure in suffering itself.

She also kills the sound mage during this rapid sequence, before he can recover and respond effectively. The refraction mage's brief dying interval is preserved rather than treating both deaths as exactly simultaneous.

### The Dying Teammate's Warning

The mortally wounded refraction mage is on his knees beside the gravity mage, bleeding out. He grips his teammate's shirt to hold himself up and looks toward him.

His fear is not only of his own death. If the remaining fighters fail to defeat Francesca, the dictator will kill the families held hostage, including his own.

His warning is expressed along these lines:

> “All our families' lives are in your hands now. My family, your family, all our families. If you lose this fight, I will never forgive you.”

He dies after delivering the warning. The gravity mage looks down at him, sweating and breathing heavily, on the verge of panic and crying.

### The Gravity Field and Suicidal Finishing Attempt

Gektor's muscles give out, leaving him on the ground, wheezing and temporarily unable to move. The gravity mage and combat mage continue fighting while he struggles to remain involved.

The combat mage begins charging a culminating [Supreme Art](../Arms%2C%20Equipment%20%26%20Abilities/magic/supreme-arts.md) above Francesca. The remaining team expects the attack to vaporize her and everyone else in the area, including themselves. They accept that prospect in the hope that killing her will save their families.

The technique takes time to charge. The gravity mage establishes an area of intensified gravity around Francesca to hold her in place until it is ready (roughly 10 quadrillion times Earth's gravity, rough estimate).

Trees, rubble, and structures inside the affected field are flattened. The ground collapses into a crater with Francesca near its center, beneath the charging magical circle.

Gektor cries out in relief when she is caught and screams at the combat mage to finish faster. His voice is furious and strained, close to breaking into tears.

#### Francesca's Slow Advance

The gravity is strong enough that Francesca must exert her full available physical strength to make progress. She can barely move, managing approximately one deliberate step per second under that effort.

This moment of genuine exertion does not mean that she has been fighting at full effort throughout the encounter. Her earlier restraint has allowed the team to reach a situation in which its restraint technique imposes a substantial physical obstacle.

The sequence holds on the effort rather than skipping directly to her escape. An initial low shot follows a foot lifting and setting down, showing the entire slow step. Later frontal shots show her body continuing forward. The intended sequence involves no more than roughly six steps, with reactions from the team as her progress becomes undeniable.

The gravity mage struggles to sustain his extreme output. Over the tense sequence, his concentration deteriorates, his eyes suffer severe damage and bleed, and he develops heavy nosebleeding. He keeps straining and shouting to hold the field despite his body's failure.

The combat mage sweats and struggles to complete the attack quickly enough. Gektor continues urging him on from the ground.

#### Why She Grins

Through the Eyes of Epiphany, Francesca can observe the gravity field and the charging magical circle. She calculates the time needed for each step, the distance to the field's edge, and the time remaining before the finishing attack is ready.

She concludes that she will leave the field before the attack can finish charging.

As she advances, her expression shifts into a wide-eyed, tooth-baring grin. It is unsettling but less exaggerated than the later monstrous presentation in Gektor's perception. Her slightly lowered glasses leave part of her eyes visible.

The grin reflects her confidence in the calculation. She is not effortlessly ignoring the gravity: she understands that, despite the effort required, their timing is insufficient to kill her.

She reaches the edge and escapes before the attack is complete. The gravity mage and combat mage subsequently die, leaving Gektor alone. Their exact individual finishing blows have not yet been specified and are not invented here.

#### Author-Only Scale Reference

The gravity field is described for authorial reference as approximately **10 quadrillion times Earth's gravity** at the mage's extreme output. Characters do not announce that number, and the audience is not given it as an on-screen statistic.

Likewise, descriptions such as “200%” communicate extraordinary exertion in the planning explanation; they do not by themselves establish a formally defined power multiplier or dialogue that must appear in the scene.

### Gektor's Final Charge: Intended Presentation

With the other team members gone, Gektor forces his exhausted body into one final stand. Rage and desperation keep him fighting despite the risk that his legs will give out.

He unleashes his strongest attack at point-blank range with everything he can force out of himself. The displaced air is so violent that nearby trees almost topple or tear free from the ground.

Francesca knocks the attack aside with one hand without flinching or taking a scratch from that exchange.

The sequence then gradually pushes the camera toward Gektor's face.

It begins with a framing that includes his head and shoulders. As it moves closer, his internal assessment progresses through the ideas:

- Speed comparable to theirs.
- Strength comparable to theirs.
- Endurance comparable to theirs.

These are his assessments within the scene, not an independent numerical ruling that Francesca's maximum abilities equal the team's.

The framing tightens until it enters the eye. The sequence then presents Francesca in an exaggerated, monstrous visual form from the threatened combatants' perspective.

The darkened features, intense eyes, and distorted presentation communicate what she feels like to them. **She has not literally transformed, revealed a separate species, or activated a magical form.**

Gektor realizes that **he is going to die**. Nevertheless, he charges.

After the brief monstrous presentation, the scene cuts directly to Gektor opening his mouth to shout and launching himself forward. They clash at the center.

They lock into a sustained close-range clash. Gektor shouts in rage, his eyes glassy, struggling to overpower her and break her guard. Francesca holds her position while he strains against her.

She tells him:

> “If you let your emotions cloud your thinking, you'll lose.”

He does not take the advice or regain his composure. She overpowers him and sends him crashing into a wall.

### The Final Clash

After more desperate fighting against Francesca, with his family and everybody else's families lives on the line.

By this stage, Gektor has transformed and is covered in magical armor that conceals his body and face.

He commits himself far beyond his normal limits. His attack meets Francesca’s outstretched hand, and she adopts a defensive stance to prevent its force from pushing her backward.

The clash continues for roughly ten seconds or longer. Neither advances. Gektor strains to break her stance, screaming in rage and desperation, while the force drives their hair backward.

His magical armor begins to fail. Cracks spread from the hands gripping his weapon, and pieces shatter and blow away along his arms and toward his neck. His helmet finally breaks away, revealing his mouth wide open as he screams, with tears streaming down his face.

Francesca smiles again, but for a different reason than during the gravity-field sequence. Her earlier calculations had not predicted that Gektor could sustain this output for so long. Based on her understanding of his condition, his body should no longer be intact and capable of producing enough force to require this defensive stance.

His continued resistance exceeds her prediction. Her smile responds to that unexpected persistence; it does not mean she can abandon her stance without being pushed back.

As Gektor’s expression intensifies, the image becomes increasingly washed out. The fully rendered scene transitions into a white background with only black outlines defining the characters. Those lines then fade and dissolve until the frame is entirely white, conveying an overwhelming, blinding intensity.

The decisive action is not shown.

When the image returns, Gektor lies unconscious on the ground. Francesca walks away, believing him probably dead.

He has survived, although his injuries are devastating. The encounter ends without revealing exactly how the final clash was resolved.
