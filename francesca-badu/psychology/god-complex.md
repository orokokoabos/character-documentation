# Does Francesca Badu have a god complex?

She possesses a high degree of self-awareness regarding their own judgments, decisions, emotions, and exercise of power, but whose self-awareness does not necessarily translate into restraint.

This person does not inherently believe that they are superior to others, that their opinions are objectively correct, or that their judgments possess greater intellectual or moral validity than anyone else's.

Francesca has many values, for example, she believes that your physical appearance determines you as a person, if you are ugly, you are not considered human, if you are attractive you are human. People who are more attractive have more superiority over others, for example, if I murder somebody, and that person is more attractive than me, then I have committed a "sin" because I am ugly and do not deserve to kill somebody more attractive than me, however if I am more attractive, then it's okay to kill this person, because I am more attractive, and therefore I am more of a human and have authority over the lower "sub humans". This is an example of one of her personal values.

However, she recognizes that this is completely normative and subjective. She acknowledges and knows that this is not objectively true, she has no way to measure or test scientifically if there even is an objective property of reality that determines "person X is more deserving over person Y". How do we measure this? What is "deserving". How do we know "deserving" in this context exists objectively. We cannot test this (as of now with our current technology and knowledge as society), therefore Francesca does not say she is correct. There is possibility and plausibility. Francesca says there is a possibility that she is correct, but it's not plausible that she is correct. These are her personal values, and she recognizes that these are not objectively correct, and she could be completely wrong. In fact, she actually believes a lot of her personal values are wrong, or have no objective say or are very weak. This specific value about attractiveness, she believes is incorrect or unsupported, and logically believes it is not correct and completely subjective, however her logical thinking, is different from her emotional thinking. She recognizes everything completely wrong with her judgements and opinions, however that does not translate action, her emotions determine action more.

This recognition does not prevent them from acting upon those judgments or imposing them on other people.

Their reasoning is not, "I am right, therefore you must obey me." Rather, it is, "I recognize that I may be wrong. I recognize that your perspective may be equally valid or even more accurate than mine. Nevertheless, this is what I personally feel, believe, or desire, and if I possess the power to enforce it, I will prioritize my own judgment over yours."

What makes this particularly distinctive is the separation between **epistemic humility and the exercise of authority**.

They possess epistemic humility in the sense that they acknowledge the limitations of their knowledge and do not necessarily consider their beliefs to be objectively true. Yet they may lack corresponding behavioral humility because their recognition of uncertainty does not meaningfully limit their willingness to exercise control over others.

Their sense of authority is therefore not necessarily rooted in a conviction of superiority, infallibility, or moral righteousness. Instead, it is rooted in the prioritization of their own subjective experience.

They do not necessarily believe that their feelings *should* be universally accepted as correct. They simply treat those feelings as sufficient grounds for their own actions, even when those actions determine what other people are permitted to do.

This creates a form of dominance that does not depend on intellectual arrogance. The person can acknowledge another individual's autonomy, recognize the legitimacy of opposing perspectives, understand that their own decisions may be unreasonable or unfair, and still choose to enforce those decisions.

The central issue is not an inability to distinguish subjective opinion from objective truth. They may understand that distinction perfectly well. The issue is that **they do not necessarily consider objective correctness, fairness, or mutual agreement to be prerequisites for exercising power**.

Their self-awareness can coexist with selfishness, emotional impulsivity, entitlement, or deliberate control. However, whether these qualities are actually present depends on their motivations, the circumstances, and the consequences of their behavior.

This is why describing the person as having a traditional "God complex" may be misleading. A God complex commonly implies an exaggerated belief in one's superiority, exceptional authority, or infallibility. The person being described may reject all of those beliefs while still behaving in a controlling or authoritarian manner.

The distinction is that they are not necessarily claiming to possess superior truth. They are allowing their personal preferences to prevail regardless of whether those preferences are justified.

**Ultimately, this is a person who can recognize that having power does not make them right, yet still allows having power to determine whose judgment prevails.**

They do not necessarily confuse power with truth. They simply choose to exercise power independently of truth.
