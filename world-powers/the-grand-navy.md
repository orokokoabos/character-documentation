# The Grand Navy

## Overview

The **Grand Navy** is a large peacekeeping and law-enforcement organization worldwide. Its stated role is to maintain peace and order, enforce the law, and oppose criminals and evildoers. 

Its personnel operate from multiple bases and headquarters and undertake missions aboard Navy ships or away from those bases. It has a chain of command through which senior officers direct personnel and authorize operations.

## Bases, Crews, and Missions

The Navy maintains multiple bases. References to a Navy headquarters do not necessarily mean a single headquarters serving the entire organization; the setting includes individual bases from which local personnel and crews operate.

Bases provide places for personnel to live, eat, receive assignments, and prepare for missions. The base featured in [Edward's Arc](../arcs/edwards-arc.md), for example, includes accommodation and a cafeteria. Much of that arc takes place there between missions.

## Rank and Institutional Authority

The Navy has an established hierarchy, including Captains, Admirals, and higher-ups with authority over operational decisions.

An officer's institutional rank is distinct from the combat classifications described in [Power Ranks](../power-ranks.md). A Navy title indicates a position within the organization; it should not automatically be treated as a particular combat rank or a guarantee of identical strength among officers with the same title.

The institution records officers' accomplishments. Defeating powerful, recognizable criminals can become part of an officer's official record. This feature is particularly relevant to Francesca's interest in documented achievements and the reputation associated with them.

Major operational decisions can require authorization from superior officers. In Edward's arc, the higher-ups control whether his crew may mobilize a Navy ship to pursue pirates after the assigned mission has been declared completed.

## Jurisdiction and Relations With Nations

**The Grand Navy does not automatically possess unrestricted jurisdiction over every nation.** Its ability to operate within another kingdom can depend on cooperation with that kingdom.

The Kingdom of Tarmi illustrates this distinction. After Edward and his supporters desert, the Navy can seek Tarmi's cooperation in capturing them. However, Tarmi hears their explanation, refuses to cooperate in their capture, and permits them to remain in the kingdom.

The Navy's ability to cooperate with a nation should therefore be distinguished from possessing unilateral authority over that nation's territory. The full arrangements governing its relations with individual nations have not yet been documented.

## Peacekeeping Ideals and Institutional Decisions

The Navy's stated purpose is important to how its members understand their service. Edward and his supporters regard themselves as people who fight for peace, order, and the protection of others.

However, those ideals do not mean that every institutional decision satisfies every member's understanding of that purpose.

In Edward's arc, pirates capture a small number of his crew members. The higher-ups refuse to authorize a pursuit, regarding the original mission as completed and further mobilization as an unacceptable escalation for the sake of those captives. Peaceful negotiations fail to obtain their release.

Edward and his crew appeal for assistance and gather support within the base. The leadership shuts down their protests as a distraction from Navy duties. When the dispute spreads, the Navy holds a press conference defending its position.

Edward and approximately two dozen supporters ultimately desert, take Navy ships without authorization, and rescue the captives themselves. The Navy labels them criminals. They later obtain refuge in Tarmi and establish [Foxden Co.](./organizations-and-entities/foxden.md).

This conflict establishes a particular clash between the Navy's leadership and members who believe it has abandoned people it ought to protect. It does not, by itself, describe every Navy officer, base, or decision throughout the wider story.

The Fleet Admiral, when the position is occupied, commands all four Admirals. The Navy can operate without an incumbent Fleet Admiral.

There has only been 2 Fleet Admirals in history, one a century ago during a previous war not explored in the story, but mentioned/referenced. People know about it. The other is [Fleet Admiral Horus](../other-characters/horus.md). He's the 2nd fleet admiral in history, and is employed by the Navy Board of Military Chiefs as the commanding leader, uniting the 4 Admirals (the admirals at the time this happened) and all their units, in order to fight the [Glyph Empire](./glyph-empire.md) during one of the major war arcs in the entire series: [Glyph vs Navy](../arcs/glyph-vs-navy.md).

### King Admiral

**King Admiral** is a historical, largely ceremonial office rather than the Navy's effective supreme operational command. Its continued existence reflects institutional tradition, and it carries little practical power.

### Board of Military Chiefs

The Grand Navy is governed by a **Board of Military Chiefs**, a governing body separate from the normal rank hierarchy.

All four Admirals automatically hold seats on the board. The Fleet Admiral, when there is one, also automatically holds a seat, as does the King Admiral.

The King Admiral's board membership does not make the office the Navy's effective ruler. Institutional governance rests with the board collectively.

### Rank, Combat Ability, and Accomplishments

Navy rank is distinct from the combat classifications described in [Power Ranks](../power-ranks.md). An institutional title does not automatically correspond to a particular combat rank or guarantee identical strength among officers holding the same title.

## History

### Holy Meekaz and Its Protector Role

The Grand Navy originated as a kingdom called **Holy Meekaz**.

Meekaz regarded protection as a central part of its role. It maintained treaties with many nations and stationed military forces around the world to protect them.

However, this extensive deployment created a weakness at home. A large proportion of its soldiers were stationed elsewhere, leaving the forces defending Meekaz itself at a substantial disadvantage.

### Glyph's Attack and the Loss of Allied Support

During the rise of the [Albagliff Empire](./glyph-empire.md), commonly shortened to the Glyph Empire, Meekaz became one of the kingdoms targeted by its expansion.

Glyph attacked Meekaz and assassinated its king.

Meekaz's military commitments abroad had left its homeland comparatively vulnerable. Its international relationships also failed to provide the support it needed: frightened by Glyph's growing power, many nations broke their treaties with Meekaz and cooperated with the empire instead.

The attack therefore left Meekaz facing both a powerful enemy and the abandonment of former allies.

Glyph's criminal origins, territorial expansion, and eventual development into an institutionalized empire are explained in [its own history](./glyph-empire.md).

### An Unresolved Grievance

Meekaz retained a deep grievance over the attack, the assassination of its king, and the betrayal of its former allies. However, it lacked the military strength and allied support necessary to retaliate.

It could not act simply because it wanted revenge.

Over time, Meekaz grew stronger. By then, however, Glyph had consolidated its empire and established relationships and treaties with other powers.

The obstacle to retaliation had changed. Meekaz was no longer confronting only the military imbalance of the original attack. Attacking Glyph could now bring it into conflict with the empire's allies as well.

Consequently, Meekaz's recovery did not provide a straightforward opportunity to settle the grievance. The hostility remained unresolved.

### Transformation Into the Grand Navy

Holy Meekaz eventually rebranded as the **Grand Navy**.

Its peacekeeping identity developed from the kingdom's existing protector role. Meekaz had already maintained forces abroad to defend other nations; the Navy continued that broader commitment to protection, law enforcement, and opposition to criminal activity.

Its experience with Glyph also gave that work a particular historical significance. Criminal groups had grown into a conquering power capable of attacking Meekaz and killing its king. Suppressing such activity could help prevent another power from developing in the same way.

The Grand Navy therefore emerged from an existing kingdom with an established military and a history of international protection. Its formation was a transformation of that institution.

### International Standing After the Transformation

The modern Grand Navy does not rely on the same extensive protective treaty network that characterized Holy Meekaz. It is broadly accepted as an international peacekeeping organization.

That acceptance should not be confused with unrestricted authority over every nation. Its ability to operate within another state's territory remains distinct from that state's willingness to cooperate with it.

### Historical Memory Within the Navy

The grievance against Glyph survives within the Grand Navy's institutional memory.

Personnel are taught the history of Glyph's attack and what happened to Holy Meekaz. Over generations, that history has also become part of the Navy's military culture.

This does not mean that every contemporary Navy member personally hates Glyph or experiences the original grievance as an individual desire for revenge. Many personnel did not experience those events and do not hold a personal grudge.

Nevertheless, the history remains familiar and institutionally significant. Individual attitudes can vary while the organization continues to preserve a collective memory of what Glyph did.

### Present Relations With Glyph

The Navy's general willingness to provide peacekeeping services does not extend to the Glyph Empire.

It avoids providing those services within the empire and does not treat Glyph as an ordinary recipient of its protection.

This exceptional relationship reflects the unresolved history between the two powers. Glyph's development into an established state did not erase its attack on Meekaz or reconcile the institutions that survived it.
