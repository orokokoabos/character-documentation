# Supreme Arts

## Overview and Scope

A **Supreme Art** forcibly imposes its user's **personal law** on reality within an established **jurisdiction**. The law determines what the art does; the jurisdiction determines where, to whom, or under what conditions it applies.

Supreme Arts represent the pinnacle of practiced magical and combat technique. Being able to use ordinary magic—even very powerful ordinary magic—is not enough to perform one.

Read the [Magic System](./magic-system.md) first, especially its explanations of requesting mana and the particles' intelligent interpretation of intent. This document uses that underlying explanation to describe what Supreme Arts actually do. **Ordinary characters do not know about the external mana sphere or consciously address it.** Their practical techniques work without that knowledge.

This entry explains general mechanics. Individual arts' full effects, character-specific breakthroughs, and battle choreography belong in their respective event documentation. In particular, it does not explain the individual Liberated Supreme Arts used by Francesca, Malu, or the sequel antagonist.

## Core Components

| Component | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Activation | The practiced gesture and spoken invocation that accompany execution |
| Anchor | The user or suitable object that receives the supplied mana |
| Request | The exceptionally precise magical intent interpreted by the mana system |
| Jurisdiction | The area, person, targets, or conditions to which the law applies |
| Personal law | The rule imposed on reality within that jurisdiction |

For an ordinary Supreme Art, the functional sequence is:

1. The user performs the activation with an established anchor.
2. The practiced intent constitutes a request for mana.
3. Mana is supplied to the anchor.
4. The requested jurisdiction is established.
5. The personal law is imposed within that jurisdiction.

Jurisdiction and the intended law are already specified in the request. Their implementation occurs after mana is supplied. The sequence is an explanation of distinct functional stages, not a requirement that each stage take a long or visibly separate interval.

## Requests: Precise Intent, Not Reciting Instructions to the Sphere

### Ordinary Magic and Supreme Arts

When someone intends to make a fireball, that intention itself can constitute a request. They do not need to think, “I am asking an external sphere to send particles.”

Supreme Arts operate through the same underlying relationship, but require vastly more precise intent and control. A casual description such as “my sword always hits living things in this room” is only an illustration of a possible law and jurisdiction. It is not a sufficiently developed Supreme Art request.

The user must become exceptionally proficient at specifying and forming the art, including its jurisdiction and law, with the precision necessary for the system to implement it.

### Automatic Mastery

Memorizing an explanation and being able to recite it accurately is insufficient.

The request must be so thoroughly practiced that its complete, precise intent can be executed automatically, without slowly reconstructing it or mentally working through a paragraph. It is comparable to a deeply ingrained skill: the trained operation is immediately available.

At activation, the detailed request must occur together with the gesture and invocation. A user cannot pause in the middle of a high-speed fight to consciously assemble every part.

Descriptions of “instructions” are an explanatory model for what the system implements. They do not require the caster to think in a particular language, consciously recite literal words internally, or understand the system's true architecture.

**The precision belongs to the intended operation, not merely the wording used to describe it.** Mana interprets intent. That interpretive ability does not remove the requirement that the intent itself be sufficiently exact.

### Why Demons Often Have an Advantage

Demonic traditions frequently emphasize prayer and requests for power. As explained in [Demon Magic](./magic-system.md#7-demon-magic), these practices can unknowingly develop the ability to request mana.

That background can give demons an advantage in the demanding request aspect of Supreme Arts. It does not mean every demon can perform one or that human practitioners cannot reach the same level.

## Rarity and Specialization

Supreme Arts are extraordinarily rare. Someone who does not repeatedly encounter the very strongest people in the world is exceedingly unlikely to meet a practitioner.

A user normally develops **one Supreme Art**. Learning it requires such extensive, focused practice that maintaining multiple distinct arts is generally beyond what a person's mind and training can sustain.

Multiple-art users exist, but they are exceptional rather than the normal next step after learning a first art. Intelligence, precision, and sustained mastery matter; merely possessing enough magical power does not make multiple arts practical.

Francesca and Malu each have only one Supreme Art in the main story, as specified under [Liberated Supreme Arts](#liberated-supreme-arts). Their later ability to use multiple arts belongs to the [sequel](../../sequel-story/), not to earlier versions of either character.

## Anchors and Activation

### The Anchor

An anchor receives the mana used to execute the Supreme Art.

Common anchors are:

- The user's own body.
- A suitable object, such as a weapon capable of receiving magical energy.

For example, a weapon incorporating [Accialite](../achilite.md), or another material capable of receiving magic, may be used as an anchor.

The anchor is **not the jurisdiction**. A user may receive mana through their own body while imposing the law on the surrounding area. A weapon serving as an anchor does not automatically make that weapon the art's sole target.

### Gesture and Invocation

Activation requires a gesture together with the spoken words **“Supreme Art”** and the art's name.

The gesture need not be made with bare hands. It can involve a held weapon or another object. The precise trained request must execute automatically in coordination with that activation.

The gesture and spoken name do not substitute for the required mastery of the request.

### Francesca's Limitation

Francesca cannot ordinarily request mana and cannot use conventional magic (there are a lot of people who are unable to use magic). Giving her a magic-receiving weapon does not, by itself, solve that inability.

Her eventual endgame Supreme Art uses an exceptional arrangement that will be explained in the relevant arc. Its anchor mechanism is deliberately not explained here.

The existence of that later arrangement does not give teenage Francesca, the Francesca of the G-Rank Free For All, or every other version of her the ability to use a Supreme Art.

## Jurisdiction

A jurisdiction specifies what falls under the art's law. It can be spatial, personal, directed at another target, or conditional. A law does not automatically apply beyond its jurisdiction merely because the user wants it to.

### Area Jurisdiction

An area-based jurisdiction might cover the room containing the user or the immediate battlefield around them.

The request must identify and establish that area accurately enough to work. Its practical reliability depends on training in the relevant kinds of surroundings.

A user who has only practiced in one room may fail when attempting the same art outdoors. Someone whose practiced description assumes a forest may fail in a city containing none of the expected trees, soil, or vegetation.

This does not require memorizing every individual location in the world. **A familiar type of environment can be sufficient.** Two forests on opposite sides of the world need not be identical for training in forest environments to transfer.

Practitioners therefore train across many types of location—forests, cities, snowy terrain, and others—so that they can establish the local jurisdiction quickly and accurately during actual fights.

Practice must develop more than a fixed description of one familiar room. It must support the precise intent appropriate to the surroundings in which the user activates.

### Self Jurisdiction

A self-based art imposes its law on the user.

This is comparatively convenient to practice because the jurisdiction remains the same person across different locations. The user does not have to rebuild an area-based description whenever the battlefield changes.

For example, an art whose law heals the user may establish the user as its jurisdiction and impose healing on that body. This is an illustrative example, not a complete specification of a particular character's art or its healing limits.

### Target Jurisdiction

An art may instead establish a particular opponent, or multiple targets, as its jurisdiction.

This is exceptionally difficult. The user must describe the intended target sufficiently precisely for the request to establish that person as the jurisdiction. Different opponents present different identification problems.

The required understanding can extend to an exceptionally detailed grasp of the opponent. Reproducing that precision immediately against unfamiliar people demands extraordinary intelligence and practice.

A vague request that fails to identify the opponent accurately may not establish the intended jurisdiction at all.

Consequently, the overwhelming majority of practitioners use area or self jurisdictions. Direct opponent-based arts are extraordinarily unusual and generally impractical for most users.

### Conditional Jurisdiction

A jurisdiction can be defined through a condition.

Examples include:

- Anyone who hears the user announce the Supreme Art.
- Anyone who sees the activation gesture.

In such cases, meeting the specified condition establishes inclusion. These examples describe how conditional selection works; they do not supply the full requests or personal laws of particular arts.

## Personal Laws and Examples

The personal law determines the operation imposed once jurisdiction is established.

### Self-Healing

If the jurisdiction is the user and the law causes healing, the art imposes that effect on the user.

The jurisdiction identifies the subject; the law determines what happens to that subject.

### Court of Blood

**Court of Blood** is an area-based Supreme Art belonging to a protagonist whose wider story is not documented here because it is unrelated to Francesca.

Its user practices across different environment types to make the area jurisdiction reliable.

The art constructs a court-like setting, including a judge and blood descending from above. Within its jurisdiction, the user controls the blood inside the bodies of those included in the court.

It is among the most dangerous Supreme Arts in the series. This entry does not establish additional trial procedures, verdict requirements, or other mechanics beyond that description.

### Prohibiting a Target's Magic

Another protagonist has a rare target-based art that prevents its opponent from using magic.

The request must identify that opponent precisely enough to establish the person as its jurisdiction. The law then imposes the inability to use magic on that target.

This illustrates a change imposed on the target's relation to magic, rather than a general removal of magic from the whole surrounding world. Its duration and other character-specific details are not established here.

## Mental Strain and Combat Risk

Using a Supreme Art imposes an enormous processing burden.

Even a skilled practitioner can suffer a brief period of mental shock or incapacitation lasting a few seconds. This is not necessarily permanent brain damage or unconsciousness; the danger is a temporary inability to respond effectively.

Against extraordinarily fast opponents, a few seconds can be fatal.

An unsuccessful activation can still impose that strain. Failure does not guarantee that the user avoids the cost simply because the intended law never takes effect.

This makes confidence in the request, jurisdiction, opponent, and circumstances essential. The art is not automatically a safe finishing move.

## Refusal Writs

### Function and Timing

**Refusal Writs** are defensive techniques that exploit the interval before an ordinary Supreme Art's jurisdiction has been established.

The stages can occur extremely quickly, but there is still a distinction between a jurisdiction not yet established and one already established.

If the defender responds in time, they make a separate magical request whose interpreted effect is to exclude them as a valid subject of the impending jurisdiction.

This is **not overpowering the Supreme Art**. The defender is being skipped by its jurisdiction rather than defeating its personal law through greater force.

A successful use produces a white-and-black outline around the defender.

### Intent and Different Traditions

Practitioners do not consciously send the sphere a message saying, “I am not a valid jurisdiction target.” They do not know about the sphere.

Instead, different defensive practices produce an intent that the mana system interprets in that functional way. The particles translate the practiced intent into an exclusion and the particles establishing the incoming jurisdiction recognize that exclusion.

Different societies and teachers may regard their versions as different techniques or argue about which is better. Their appearances and methods can differ while the underlying mechanism remains the same.

The decisive requirement is sufficiently precise intent and execution. No particular language or literal phrasing is required, and elaborate words alone do not establish a successful refusal.

### Knowledge and Limitations

A defender needs some understanding of the opponent and the relevant incoming art. The required specificity is less demanding than that of a Supreme Art directly targeting an opponent, but refusal is not guaranteed to succeed.

Refusal can exclude a person from an area-based jurisdiction or an art directly targeting them. It does not work against every art.

It does not counter a law the opponent imposes on their own body: the defender was never the subject of that self-based jurisdiction.

The exact treatment of every conditional jurisdiction is not established by these general examples. No universal protection against all conditional arts should be inferred.

### The Opening Created by Refusal (Example Danger)

A caster may undergo mental shock while the defender is successfully excluded.

The result can be particularly dangerous: the defender remains unaffected and able to act while the caster is briefly incapacitated.

A successful refusal is therefore capable of turning the attempted use of an extraordinary offensive technique into a fatal opening.

## People Unable to Use Magic

An ordinary Supreme Art is **likely, but not guaranteed**, to overlook someone who has no capacity to use magic.

Its jurisdiction may recognize magic-capable people as participants in the mana system while treating a non-user more like an object for selection purposes.

This is a classification used in implementing the jurisdiction. It is not a claim that the person is literally nonliving or lacks a soul.

For example, an area may contain one magic user and one non-user. An ordinary request can include the magic user while failing to include the non-user as a person to whom its law should apply.

**Salore cannot use magic.** A practitioner who assumes their Supreme Art will necessarily include him can suffer the mental cost of activation while leaving him unaffected and free to attack.

This is not absolute immunity. A sufficiently specific request can include someone unable to use magic.

It also does not establish universal immunity to the physical consequences of surrounding events. The selection issue concerns whether the personal law directly includes the person under the jurisdiction.

## Overlapping Ordinary Supreme Arts

Simultaneous activation does not automatically cause Supreme Arts to cancel each other.

When ordinary jurisdictions overlap, their competing claims are resolved primarily through the **specificity of their requests**.

A more specific request takes precedence over a less specific request in the jurisdictional conflict. Raw power alone is not the stated resolution rule.

If the requests have approximately equal specificity, the art that activates first takes priority. The relevant timing window is extremely small, so genuinely simultaneous activation is rare.

No additional tie-breaker has yet been established for requests that are equally specific and truly simultaneous.

### Examples

| Combination | Jurisdictional result |
| --- | --- |
| Two users each impose an art on themselves | Their jurisdictions do not overlap merely because they activate together |
| One user imposes a law on themselves; another targets that same person | Both claim jurisdiction over that person, producing a conflict |
| Two area-based jurisdictions overlap | The overlap creates a jurisdictional conflict resolved through request specificity, then timing where specificity is approximately equal |

The request must therefore be practiced not only well enough to function, but precisely enough to hold priority against another Supreme Art.

### The G-Rank Free For All

The [G-Rank Free For All](../../arcs/glyph-vs-navy.md#the-g-rank-free-for-all) reaches a rare moment of simultaneous Supreme Art activation.

Francesca's physical disturbance of the cave interrupts the combatants' execution. The detailed account remains in the arc document; this moment does not establish a completed clash whose winner must be invented from the general priority rules.

## Liberated Supreme Arts

### No Jurisdiction

A **Liberated Supreme Art** imposes its personal law on reality itself **without establishing a jurisdiction**.

It is not merely an ordinary art with an exceptionally large area. The jurisdiction stage itself is absent.

The normal model is therefore altered at the crucial point: the art does not first delimit a jurisdiction and then impose its law within it.

This is an unprecedented development relative to established Supreme Art practice. It is not something ordinary practitioners had already imagined as a normal possibility.

### Refusal and Coexistence

Refusal Writs do not work against a Liberated Supreme Art, because there is no jurisdiction from which to exclude the defender.

The absence of jurisdiction also means that Liberated Arts do not enter the jurisdictional clashes described above.

An ordinary Supreme Art and a Liberated Supreme Art can operate at the same time. Two Liberated Supreme Arts can likewise operate simultaneously. Neither combination automatically cancels an art or triggers the ordinary specificity contest through jurisdictional overlap.

This establishes coexistence without a jurisdictional clash. It does not provide an additional, invented rule for every conceivable interaction between the effects of their individual laws.

## Invention and Known Practitioners

### Endgame Francesca: The First Liberated Supreme Art

**Endgame Francesca, during the final fights of the main story**, invents the first Liberated Supreme Art while fighting Demon King Malu.

This feat belongs specifically to that version of Francesca under the circumstances of that confrontation. It is not an ability she possessed throughout the main story or could simply have used during the G-Rank Free For All.

The breakthrough does not mean that she already knows the origin of mana, the external sphere, or the Researcher. Her full discovery of the mana system remains a sequel development.

### Malu: Immediate Replication

In Malu's original era, Supreme Arts did not exist. He has never encountered the technique before witnessing Francesca's Liberated Art.

He nevertheless understands what she has done and immediately creates his own Liberated Supreme Art during the same fight.

The first two Liberated Arts are thus invented within one confrontation: Francesca produces an unprecedented advancement beyond the already exceptional ordinary Supreme Art, and Malu grasps and reproduces the principle on the spot.

The individual laws of those two arts are deliberately not explained here. Their effects belong in the documentation of that fight.

### Collective Reconstruction and Francesca's Imprisonment

The world witnesses the fight and the appearance of arts with no jurisdiction.

Over the following months, others work to reverse-engineer the principle. Their eventual implementation requires **tens of thousands of priests, monks, and mages** acting together.

They execute a collective Liberated Supreme Art that traps Francesca.

This demonstrates that multiple people can jointly perform a Supreme Art when their requests, actions, and execution are synchronized. The collective imprisonment is the only established example here of such a group performance; it should not be treated as a common alternative to individual training.

Francesca's invention therefore helps enable her own defeat. By demonstrating that a Liberated Art is possible, she gives others the principle they later reconstruct and use against her. Without her demonstration, it is considered highly unlikely that anyone would independently develop one under those circumstances.

### The Sequel Antagonist

A principal antagonist in the sequel also possesses a Liberated Supreme Art. The antagonist's identity and the art's individual law are not specified here.

For the separate period and its available context, see the [Sequel Story](../../sequel-story/).

### Counting Practitioners, Collective Users, and Arts

That is not a count of four individual people, nor a limit of four distinct Liberated Arts.

In the main story, Francesca and Malu each have one art. In the sequel, both become capable of multiple Supreme Arts, including multiple Liberated Arts. Their sequel capabilities must not be attributed to their earlier main-story versions.

The individual effects of those later arts belong in the [sequel's event documentation](../../sequel-story/sequel-story-events/), rather than being invented or exhaustively described in this general reference.
